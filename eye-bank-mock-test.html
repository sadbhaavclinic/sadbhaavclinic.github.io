Eye Banking Mock Test 1 (Mixed): 30 MCQs with Answers

For: Allied eye-care staff

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • 30 original questions covering the eye banking study course.
  • Answer all, then press "Check my score": correct answers turn green, wrong ones red.
  • Open "Show answer" for the explanation.
  • Revise with the study course.

Study courses for eye-care careers: Ophthalmic Nursing · Orthoptics · Ophthalmic Dispensing · Ocularistry · Eye Banking · Ophthalmic Photography and Imaging · Low Vision and Vision Rehabilitation · Eye Care Support Roles · Ophthalmic technician · All careers

Set a timer for 30 minutes. The option order is mixed, so read every choice.

1. Corneal donation is ideally done within:

Show answer

Answer: 4–6 hours of death. Earlier retrieval preserves endothelial cells.

2. Who can donate eyes?

Show answer

Answer: People of almost any age, including those with glasses, cataract surgery or diabetes. Medical suitability is checked by the eye bank.

3. A contraindication to corneal donation is:

Show answer

Answer: Rabies. Also HIV, hepatitis B/C, septicaemia and others per standards.

4. The body of a donor while waiting for the eye bank team should have:

Show answer

Answer: Eyes closed, head raised, fan off, wet cotton over lids if possible. Reduces drying and swelling.

5. In-situ corneoscleral excision means:

Show answer

Answer: Removing only the cornea with a scleral rim. Preserves the face appearance; common in hospital retrieval.

6. Specular microscopy assesses:

Show answer

Answer: Endothelial cell density and morphology. Key for graft suitability.

7. A typical minimum endothelial cell density for penetrating keratoplasty tissue is around:

Show answer

Answer: 2,000 cells/mm² (varies by standard). Each eye bank follows its medical standards.

8. MK medium allows corneal storage for about:

Show answer

Answer: 4 days at 4 °C. Intermediate media allow longer; organ culture longer still.

9. Organ culture storage is done at about:

Show answer

Answer: 31–37 °C. Allows weeks of storage with sterility testing.

10. Glycerol-preserved corneas are suitable for:

Show answer

Answer: Tectonic (structural) grafts. No living endothelium.

11. Serology is tested on:

Show answer

Answer: Donor blood sample. For HIV, HBV, HCV and other markers per standards.

12. The Indian law governing eye donation is:

Show answer

Answer: Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act. With NOTTO coordinating at national level.

13. Hospital Cornea Retrieval Programme relies on:

Show answer

Answer: Grief counsellors approaching families in hospitals after a death. Major source of donor corneas in India.

14. Tissue for DMEK requires:

Show answer

Answer: Healthy endothelium and Descemet membrane. Endothelial keratoplasty uses the back layer.

15. Laminar flow hoods are used to:

Show answer

Answer: Maintain a sterile work area. Processing under aseptic conditions.

16. Every tissue must be traceable from:

Show answer

Answer: Donor to recipient. Essential for safety and recalls.

17. Consent for eye donation in India is usually given by:

Show answer

Answer: The next of kin (or prior pledge honoured by family). Families must agree at the time of death.

18. A myth about eye donation is that:

Show answer

Answer: It disfigures the face. Retrieval does not disfigure; cosmetic care is taken.

19. Slit lamp evaluation of donor tissue looks for:

Show answer

Answer: Epithelial defects, stromal opacities, folds, guttae. Grading determines use.

20. Biomedical waste from retrieval (blood-soaked swabs) goes into:

Show answer

Answer: Yellow bag. India's BMW rules.

21. Sclera from donor eyes may be used for:

Show answer

Answer: Patch grafts (e.g. glaucoma tube covers, scleral melts). Stored per standards.

22. A donor's eyes should not be retrieved if:

Show answer

Answer: Infection with septicaemia or contraindicated disease is present. Follow the contraindication list.

23. The Certified Eye Banker – Technical (CEBT) credential is offered by:

Show answer

Answer: Eye Bank Association of America. Includes an exam and practical competency.

24. One donor can usually help:

Show answer

Answer: Up to two (or more with split techniques) corneal transplant recipients. Each cornea can go to a different patient.

25. Tissue temperature in MK storage is maintained by:

Show answer

Answer: Refrigerator at 2–8 °C with monitoring. Temperature logs are required.

26. Time from death to retrieval should be:

Show answer

Answer: Recorded accurately along with time to preservation. Affects suitability.

27. Corneal guttae on donor tissue suggest:

Show answer

Answer: Fuchs dystrophy changes. Usually unsuitable for optical grafts.

28. An eye bank counsellor should:

Show answer

Answer: Approach with empathy, provide information and respect the decision. Ethics and law prohibit payment.

29. Before release for surgery, tissue must have:

Show answer

Answer: Complete donor screening, serology results and evaluation documented. Medical director release.

30. Hand hygiene and sterile gloves at retrieval prevent:

Show answer

Answer: Contamination and infection of tissue. Aseptic technique is essential.
