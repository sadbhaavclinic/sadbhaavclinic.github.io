Landmark Trials in Diabetic Retinopathy and DME: DRS, ETDRS, DRCR Protocols and Anti-VEGF

For: PG residents

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • DRS: PRP reduced severe visual loss by 50% or more.
  • ETDRS: Focal laser for clinically significant macular oedema halved moderate visual loss (about 24% to 12% at 3 years).
  • DRVS: Early vitrectomy gave more eyes 6/12 or better in type 1 diabetes (36% vs 12%); no benefit in type 2.
  • DCCT: Intensive control reduced the onset of retinopathy by about 76% and progression by about 54%.
  • UKPDS: Intensive glucose control reduced microvascular end points by about 25%.
  • FIELD and ACCORD Eye: FIELD: fewer patients needed first laser treatment.

Diabetic eye disease has the richest trial record in ophthalmology. The laser era (DRS, ETDRS) defined high-risk PDR and clinically significant macular oedema. Systemic trials showed that glucose and blood pressure control matter. The injection era, much of it run by the DRCR Retina Network, moved first-line treatment of DME and then of PDR towards anti-VEGF.

How each trial is set out: design and size, the question it asked, what it found, why it matters, the trap examiners like to set, and where it stands today. Numbers are rounded; check the original paper before quoting a figure in a thesis.

The laser era

DRS (1976–1981)

Diabetic Retinopathy Study

DesignPRP (argon or xenon) in one eye vs no treatment in the other · 1,758 patients with PDR or severe NPDR
QuestionDoes panretinal photocoagulation prevent blindness in PDR?
FindingPRP reduced severe visual loss by 50% or more. The benefit was greatest in eyes with high-risk characteristics.
Why it mattersDefined high-risk PDR: NVD of at least a quarter to a third of a disc area, any NVD with vitreous or preretinal haemorrhage, or NVE of at least half a disc area with haemorrhage.
Viva trapSevere visual loss here means acuity below 5/200 on two consecutive visits, not the ETDRS 15-letter definition.

ETDRS (1985–1991)

Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study

DesignFocal laser for macular oedema; early vs deferred PRP; aspirin 650 mg vs placebo · 3,711 patients
QuestionWhen should laser be given, and does aspirin help?
FindingFocal laser for clinically significant macular oedema halved moderate visual loss (about 24% to 12% at 3 years). Early PRP was not advised for mild or moderate NPDR. Aspirin had no effect on retinopathy or vitreous haemorrhage.
Why it mattersDefined CSMO, the ETDRS chart and the severity scale still used today.
Viva trapModerate visual loss means doubling of the visual angle (15 letters). The 4-2-1 rule for severe NPDR comes from ETDRS data.

DRVS (1985–1990)

Diabetic Retinopathy Vitrectomy Study

DesignEarly vitrectomy vs deferral for 1 year for severe vitreous haemorrhage (616 eyes); early vitrectomy for very severe PDR
QuestionShould severe diabetic vitreous haemorrhage be operated on early?
FindingEarly vitrectomy gave more eyes 6/12 or better in type 1 diabetes (36% vs 12%); no benefit in type 2.
Why it mattersSupported early vitrectomy in type 1 diabetes with severe haemorrhage.
Viva trapA pre-endolaser, pre-anti-VEGF trial: modern vitrectomy is safer, so the threshold for surgery is lower today.

Systemic control

DCCT (1993)

Diabetes Control and Complications Trial

DesignIntensive vs conventional insulin therapy · 1,441 people with type 1 diabetes · mean 6.5 years
QuestionDoes tight glucose control prevent retinopathy?
FindingIntensive control reduced the onset of retinopathy by about 76% and progression by about 54%.
Why it mattersTight glycaemic control became the foundation of prevention.
Viva trapEarly worsening: retinopathy can worsen in the first year after glucose control is suddenly tightened. The benefit persists for years afterwards ("metabolic memory", EDIC).

UKPDS (1998)

United Kingdom Prospective Diabetes Study

DesignIntensive vs conventional glucose control, and tight vs less tight blood pressure control · 5,102 people with type 2 diabetes
QuestionDo glucose and blood pressure control matter in type 2 diabetes?
FindingIntensive glucose control reduced microvascular end points by about 25%. Tight blood pressure control (below 150/85) reduced retinopathy progression by about a third and the need for laser.
Why it mattersBlood pressure control became part of diabetic eye care.
Viva trapRemember both arms: glucose and blood pressure.

FIELD and ACCORD Eye (2007–2010)

Fenofibrate in diabetic retinopathy

DesignFenofibrate vs placebo in type 2 diabetes (FIELD, 9,795 patients); fenofibrate plus statin in ACCORD Eye (about 2,850)
QuestionDoes a lipid-lowering drug slow retinopathy?
FindingFIELD: fewer patients needed first laser treatment. ACCORD Eye: fenofibrate with simvastatin slowed retinopathy progression (6.5% vs 10.2% over 4 years).
Why it mattersFenofibrate is considered for patients with existing retinopathy in some guidelines.
Viva trapThe benefit appeared independent of lipid levels, suggesting a direct effect.

Anti-VEGF for diabetic macular oedema

RESOLVE (2010)

Safety and efficacy of ranibizumab in DME with centre involvement

DesignRanibizumab vs sham · 151 patients · 1 year · phase II
QuestionDoes ranibizumab work in DME?
FindingMean gain about +10 letters vs a small loss with sham.
Why it mattersEarly proof of anti-VEGF in DME.
Viva trapPhase II: it showed efficacy, not the best dose or regimen.

RESTORE (2011)

Ranibizumab monotherapy or combined with laser vs laser in DME

DesignRanibizumab alone vs ranibizumab plus laser vs laser alone · 345 patients · 1 year
QuestionIs ranibizumab better than laser for visual impairment due to DME?
FindingMean gains about +6.1 (ranibizumab), +5.9 (combination) and +0.8 letters (laser).
Why it mattersLed to European approval of ranibizumab for DME.
Viva trapAdding laser did not add to vision.

RISE and RIDE (2012)

Ranibizumab for DME

DesignMonthly ranibizumab 0.3 or 0.5 mg vs sham (rescue laser allowed) · 759 patients · 2 years (3 in extension)
QuestionDoes monthly ranibizumab help DME?
FindingAbout 34–45% gained 15 letters vs 12–18% with sham at 2 years, and fewer eyes progressed to PDR.
Why it mattersUS approval of ranibizumab (0.3 mg) for DME.
Viva trapPatients switched from sham in year 3 gained less: delay costs vision.

VIVID and VISTA (2014)

Aflibercept vs laser in DME

DesignAflibercept 2 mg every 4 weeks or every 8 weeks after 5 loading doses vs laser · 872 patients · 2–3 years
QuestionIs aflibercept better than laser for DME?
FindingMean gains of about +10 to +12 letters vs about +1 with laser at 1 year, similar for 4- and 8-weekly dosing.
Why it mattersAflibercept approved for DME with 8-weekly maintenance.
Viva trapFive monthly loading doses before 8-weekly dosing, not three as in AMD.

Protocol I (2010)

DRCR: ranibizumab with prompt or deferred laser, or triamcinolone with laser, vs laser alone

DesignFour arms · 691 patients (854 eyes) · centre-involving DME · 1 year (5-year follow-up)
QuestionAre injections better than laser alone for centre-involving DME?
FindingRanibizumab with prompt or deferred laser gave about +9 letters vs +3 with laser alone. Triamcinolone plus laser helped pseudophakic eyes.
Why it mattersAnti-VEGF became first-line for centre-involving DME.
Viva trapDeferred laser gave slightly better vision than prompt laser at 5 years, and many eyes never needed laser at all.

Protocol T (2015–2016)

DRCR: aflibercept vs bevacizumab vs ranibizumab for DME

DesignThree drugs, same retreatment rules · 660 patients · 2 years
QuestionWhich anti-VEGF drug is best for DME?
FindingWith vision of 6/12 or better at baseline, all three were similar. With vision of 6/15 or worse, aflibercept gave better vision at 1 year (+19 vs +12 bevacizumab vs +14 ranibizumab); the gap with ranibizumab had narrowed by 2 years.
Why it mattersChoice of drug depends on starting vision; bevacizumab is reasonable for good starting vision.
Viva trapThe aflibercept advantage was only in eyes with worse starting vision.

Protocol V (2019)

DRCR: treatment of centre-involving DME in eyes with good vision

DesignAflibercept vs laser vs observation (with aflibercept if vision fell) · 702 eyes with vision 6/7.5 or better · 2 years
QuestionDoes centre-involving DME with good vision need immediate injections?
FindingNo: at 2 years the proportion losing 5 letters or more was similar in all three groups (about 16–19%).
Why it mattersObservation with close follow-up is reasonable when vision is good.
Viva trapObservation meant regular follow-up and prompt aflibercept if vision dropped; it is not "do nothing".

Steroids for diabetic macular oedema

Protocol B (2008)

DRCR: intravitreal triamcinolone vs focal/grid laser

DesignTriamcinolone 1 or 4 mg vs laser · 840 eyes · 2–3 years
QuestionIs triamcinolone better than laser for DME?
FindingTriamcinolone 4 mg was ahead at 4 months, the groups were level at 1 year, and laser was better at 2 years with fewer side effects.
Why it mattersLaser stayed standard until anti-VEGF; steroids kept for refractory cases.
Viva trapThis trial tested triamcinolone injections, not the sustained-release implants of MEAD and FAME.

MEAD (2014)

Dexamethasone implant in DME

DesignDexamethasone implant 0.7 or 0.35 mg vs sham · 1,048 patients · 3 years
QuestionDoes a dexamethasone implant help DME?
FindingAbout 22% (0.7 mg) vs 12% (sham) gained 15 letters. The benefit was clearer in pseudophakic eyes.
Why it mattersThe implant is used mainly in pseudophakic eyes or after poor response to anti-VEGF.
Viva trapCataract developed in most phakic eyes, temporarily erasing the visual gain.

FAME (2011–2012)

Fluocinolone acetonide in DME

DesignFluocinolone implant (0.2 or 0.5 µg/day) vs sham · 956 patients previously treated with laser · 3 years
QuestionDoes a 3-year steroid implant help persistent DME?
FindingAbout 28% vs 19% gained 15 letters at 3 years; the benefit was larger in chronic DME (more than 3 years).
Why it mattersIluvien is used for chronic DME not responding to other treatment, mainly in pseudophakic eyes.
Viva trapAlmost all phakic eyes developed cataract, and some patients needed glaucoma surgery.

Proliferative and non-proliferative retinopathy

Protocol S (2015)

DRCR: ranibizumab vs PRP for PDR

DesignRanibizumab vs PRP · 394 eyes · 2 years (5-year follow-up)
QuestionCan injections replace PRP for proliferative retinopathy?
FindingRanibizumab was non-inferior for vision at 2 years, with less peripheral field loss, less DME and fewer vitrectomies. At 5 years vision was similar in both groups.
Why it mattersAnti-VEGF became an alternative to PRP for patients who will attend reliably.
Viva trapPatients lost to follow-up on anti-VEGF alone are at risk of severe complications; PRP is permanent, injections are not.

CLARITY (2017)

Aflibercept vs PRP for PDR

DesignAflibercept vs PRP · 232 patients · UK · 1 year
QuestionIs aflibercept at least as good as PRP for vision in PDR?
FindingAflibercept was non-inferior and then superior by about 4 letters at 52 weeks, with about 4 injections in the year.
Why it mattersSupported aflibercept as an alternative to PRP.
Viva trapThe patients were milder than in Protocol S, and none had macular oedema.

PANORAMA (2021)

Aflibercept for moderately severe to severe NPDR

DesignAflibercept every 8 or 16 weeks vs sham · 402 eyes without centre-involving DME · 1–2 years
QuestionCan anti-VEGF reverse the retinopathy itself?
FindingAbout 65–80% vs 15% improved by 2 or more severity steps at 1 year, with fewer vision-threatening complications.
Why it mattersShowed that anti-VEGF can regress retinopathy severity, not only treat oedema.
Viva trapThe end point is severity steps, not vision.

Protocol W (2021–2023)

DRCR: aflibercept to prevent complications of NPDR

DesignAflibercept vs sham · 399 eyes with moderate to severe NPDR · 4 years
QuestionDoes early aflibercept prevent PDR and DME and protect vision?
FindingFewer eyes developed PDR or centre-involving DME (34% vs 57%), but vision at 4 years was the same.
Why it mattersRoutine preventive injections for NPDR are not recommended.
Viva trapRead with PANORAMA: anatomy improves, but vision does not, when patients are treated promptly once complications appear.

Protocol AB (2020)

DRCR: aflibercept vs vitrectomy with PRP for vitreous haemorrhage from PDR

DesignAflibercept vs early vitrectomy · 205 eyes · 2 years
QuestionInject or operate for diabetic vitreous haemorrhage?
FindingMean vision over 24 weeks was similar. Vision recovered faster after vitrectomy, and about a third of the aflibercept group later needed vitrectomy.
Why it mattersEither approach is acceptable; the choice depends on the patient.
Viva trapVision recovered faster with vitrectomy, which matters for patients who need to see quickly.

Protocol A (2007)

DRCR: modified ETDRS laser vs mild macular grid laser

DesignTwo laser techniques · 323 eyes · 1 year
QuestionCan macular laser be made gentler without losing effect?
FindingModified ETDRS focal/grid laser reduced OCT thickness more; vision differed only slightly.
Why it mattersMade modified ETDRS laser the comparator for later DRCR trials.
Viva trapThe primary outcome was OCT thickness, not vision.

Landmark trials by subspecialty: AMD · Retinal vein occlusion · Diabetic retinopathy and DME · Vitreoretinal and macular · Glaucoma · Cornea and cataract · Uveitis and oncology · Paediatrics and strabismus · Neuro-ophthalmology. One-line revision table: all landmark trials.
