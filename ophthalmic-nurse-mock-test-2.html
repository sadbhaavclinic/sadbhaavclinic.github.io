Ophthalmic Nursing Mock Test 2: Eye Conditions, Systemic Disease and Pharmacology (40 MCQs)

For: Allied eye-care staff

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • 40 original questions on eye conditions, systemic disease and pharmacology.
  • Answer all, then press "Check my score": correct answers turn green, wrong ones red.
  • Open "Show answer" for the explanation.
  • Revise from the course modules.

Practise: Mock 1 · Mock 2 · Mock 3 · Mock 4 · Viva and OSCE · Career guide

Set a timer for 40 minutes. The option order is mixed, so read every choice.

1. The layer of the cornea that pumps water out to keep it clear is the:

Show answer

Answer: Endothelium. Endothelial cells do not regenerate in adults.

2. Meibomian glands produce the:

Show answer

Answer: Oil layer. Blocked glands cause chalazion and evaporative dry eye.

3. Horner syndrome causes:

Show answer

Answer: Small pupil and mild ptosis. Sympathetic pathway lesion.

4. A swollen tender lump at the inner corner of the lower lid with pus suggests:

Show answer

Answer: Acute dacryocystitis. Systemic antibiotics; DCR later.

5. Hypopyon is a layer of:

Show answer

Answer: White cells (pus). Seen in severe keratitis or endophthalmitis.

6. A contact lens wearer who rinses lenses in tap water is at special risk of:

Show answer

Answer: Acanthamoeba keratitis. Very painful, hard to treat.

7. Keratoconus progression can be stopped by:

Show answer

Answer: Corneal cross-linking. Riboflavin with UV light.

8. Vernal keratoconjunctivitis typically affects:

Show answer

Answer: Boys in hot climates. Severe itching, seasonal.

9. Open-angle glaucoma typically causes early:

Show answer

Answer: Peripheral field loss without symptoms. Silent disease.

10. Neovascular glaucoma most often follows:

Show answer

Answer: Diabetic retinopathy or central retinal vein occlusion. New vessels block drainage.

11. A cherry-red spot at the macula with sudden painless loss of vision indicates:

Show answer

Answer: Central retinal artery occlusion. Emergency.

12. The Amsler grid helps patients detect early:

Show answer

Answer: Wet AMD. Distortion or missing areas.

13. Retinoblastoma most often presents with:

Show answer

Answer: White pupillary reflex. Also squint.

14. Night blindness with progressive narrowing of the visual field suggests:

Show answer

Answer: Retinitis pigmentosa. Inherited retinal dystrophy.

15. Optic neuritis is most strongly linked with:

Show answer

Answer: Multiple sclerosis. Painful eye movement.

16. Bitemporal hemianopia suggests a lesion at the:

Show answer

Answer: Optic chiasm. Often pituitary tumour.

17. In type 1 diabetes, retinal screening usually begins:

Show answer

Answer: Within 5 years of diagnosis. Then yearly.

18. Lid retraction and proptosis are classic signs of:

Show answer

Answer: Thyroid eye disease. Graves' orbitopathy.

19. Sjögren syndrome causes:

Show answer

Answer: Dry eyes and dry mouth. Autoimmune.

20. Herpes zoster with vesicles on the tip of the nose suggests:

Show answer

Answer: Likely eye involvement (Hutchinson sign). Nasociliary nerve involvement.

21. Cytomegalovirus retinitis is treated with:

Show answer

Answer: Ganciclovir or valganciclovir. Systemic or intravitreal.

22. Which drug used for prostate enlargement causes floppy iris during cataract surgery?

Show answer

Answer: Tamsulosin. Alpha-1A blocker.

23. Tropicamide's effect usually lasts about:

Show answer

Answer: 4–6 hours. Short-acting.

24. Atropine 1% dilation may last:

Show answer

Answer: Up to 1–2 weeks. Longest-acting cycloplegic.

25. Pilocarpine works by:

Show answer

Answer: Contracting the ciliary muscle and pupil sphincter, opening drainage. Cholinergic agonist.

26. Latanoprost is usually instilled:

Show answer

Answer: Once daily in the evening. Prostaglandin analogue.

27. A patient using timolol drops reports breathlessness and wheeze. The likely cause is:

Show answer

Answer: Beta-blocker induced bronchospasm. Report; review drop.

28. Oral acetazolamide should be used with caution in patients with:

Show answer

Answer: Sulfonamide allergy and kidney disease. Also low potassium.

29. Natamycin 5% is used for:

Show answer

Answer: Fungal keratitis (especially filamentary). Suspension: shake well.

30. Which steroid drop is less likely to raise IOP?

Show answer

Answer: Loteprednol or fluorometholone. Still monitor IOP.

31. Nepafenac and bromfenac are:

Show answer

Answer: NSAIDs. Used after cataract surgery.

32. Proparacaine is a:

Show answer

Answer: Topical anaesthetic. For examination only.

33. Fluorescein dye under cobalt blue light makes epithelial defects appear:

Show answer

Answer: Green. Bright green stain.

34. Mitomycin C is used in trabeculectomy to:

Show answer

Answer: Reduce scarring of the bleb. Antimetabolite.

35. Anti-VEGF agents are stored at:

Show answer

Answer: 2–8 °C. Cold chain.

36. 0.5% timolol contains:

Show answer

Answer: 5 mg/mL. 0.5 g per 100 mL.

37. A suspension drop should be given:

Show answer

Answer: After solutions, shaken well. Order of instillation.

38. Brimonidine is avoided in children under 2 years because it can cause:

Show answer

Answer: Drowsiness and breathing depression. Central nervous system effects.

39. Hyperosmotic agents lower IOP by:

Show answer

Answer: Drawing water out of the vitreous into the blood. Osmotic gradient.

40. Cyclosporine eye drops are used for:

Show answer

Answer: Dry eye disease and severe allergic eye disease. Immunomodulator.
