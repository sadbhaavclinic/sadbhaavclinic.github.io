Orthoptics Mock Test 2: Binocular Vision, Amblyopia and Examination (40 MCQs)

For: Optometrists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • 40 original questions on binocular vision, amblyopia and examination.
  • Answer all, then press "Check my score": correct answers turn green, wrong ones red.
  • Open "Show answer" for the explanation.
  • Revise from the course modules.

Orthoptics course: Course home · M1 · M2 · M3 · M4 · M5 · M6 · M7 · M8 · M9 · M10

Practise: Mock 1 · Mock 2 · Mock 3 · Viva and OSCE · Career guide

Set a timer for 40 minutes. The option order is mixed, so read every choice.

1. The inferior oblique's primary action is:

Show answer

Answer: Extorsion. Secondary elevation.

2. The superior rectus is best tested for elevation with the eye:

Show answer

Answer: Abducted. Vertical recti in abduction.

3. Panum's fusional area allows:

Show answer

Answer: Single vision of slightly disparate images, giving stereopsis. Around the horopter.

4. Physiological diplopia refers to:

Show answer

Answer: Normal doubling of objects far from the fixation plane. Used in exercises.

5. Harmonious abnormal retinal correspondence means:

Show answer

Answer: Subjective angle is zero despite a manifest deviation. Brain adapts.

6. The sensitive period for amblyopia extends roughly to:

Show answer

Answer: 7–8 years. Some plasticity later.

7. Hypermetropic anisometropia of what size commonly causes amblyopia?

Show answer

Answer: 1 D or more. Unequal blur.

8. Occlusion dose for severe amblyopia: 6 hours compared with full-time was:

Show answer

Answer: Similar. PEDIG.

9. A child strongly objects to covering the right eye but not the left. This suggests:

Show answer

Answer: Poor vision in the left eye. Objection when good eye covered.

10. Kay pictures are suited to children aged about:

Show answer

Answer: 2–3 years. Picture optotypes.

11. Matching cards help children who:

Show answer

Answer: Cannot name letters. Point to match.

12. A logMAR score of 1.0 equals:

Show answer

Answer: 6/60. 10 lines worse.

13. The cover/uncover test detects:

Show answer

Answer: Tropia. Movement of uncovered eye.

14. On uncovering, the covered eye moves outward to take up fixation. This indicates:

Show answer

Answer: Esophoria. Eye was deviated in.

15. Base-up prism before the right eye corrects a:

Show answer

Answer: Right hypotropia. Base opposite deviation.

16. Krimsky test uses prisms to:

Show answer

Answer: Centre corneal reflections. When cover test impossible.

17. The near point of convergence is normal if the break is within about:

Show answer

Answer: 6–10 cm. Remote NPC suggests CI.

18. A high AC/A ratio means:

Show answer

Answer: Excess convergence per dioptre of accommodation. Near eso.

19. Gradient AC/A is measured by:

Show answer

Answer: Changing the deviation with lenses at fixed distance. Lens method.

20. A patient sees five lights on Worth four-dot. This means:

Show answer

Answer: Diplopia. Two red + three green.

21. The TNO test uses:

Show answer

Answer: Red-green glasses and random dots. Random-dot anaglyph.

22. The Lang stereotest:

Show answer

Answer: Needs no glasses. Cylinder gratings.

23. Normal adult stereoacuity is about:

Show answer

Answer: 40–60 seconds of arc. Fine stereo.

24. The synoptophore can measure:

Show answer

Answer: Objective and subjective angles, fusion range, grades of BSV. Major amblyoscope.

25. The field of binocular single vision is plotted to:

Show answer

Answer: Show where the patient sees singly, e.g. for driving or surgery. Perimeter.

26. Sheard's criterion: a 6Δ exophoria needs a positive fusional reserve of at least:

Show answer

Answer: 12Δ. Twice the phoria.

27. Convergence insufficiency treatment with best evidence in children is:

Show answer

Answer: Office-based vergence/accommodative therapy. CITT.

28. Accommodative facility is tested with:

Show answer

Answer: ±2.00 D flipper lenses. Cycles per minute.

29. Pseudo-esotropia is commonly due to:

Show answer

Answer: Broad nasal bridge and epicanthic folds. Normal cover test.

30. The Hirschberg reflex at the limbus suggests about:

Show answer

Answer: 45°. Large deviation.

31. Eccentric fixation is assessed with:

Show answer

Answer: Visuscope or ophthalmoscope target. Star on fovea.

32. A Maddox wing measures:

Show answer

Answer: Near phoria (horizontal and vertical). At 33 cm.

33. A Maddox rod measures:

Show answer

Answer: Distance phoria (with a light). Streak and spot.

34. Microtropia with identity shows:

Show answer

Answer: No movement on cover test. Eccentric fixation point = ARC point.

35. Infants usually achieve steady alignment by about:

Show answer

Answer: 3–4 months. Intermittent misalignment earlier can be normal.

36. Refractive adaptation in amblyopia takes about:

Show answer

Answer: 12–18 weeks. Glasses alone.

37. The most important factor in patching success is:

Show answer

Answer: Compliance. Support families.

38. Dichoptic therapy presents:

Show answer

Answer: Different images to each eye to encourage binocular use. Games, films.

39. Accommodative spasm presents with:

Show answer

Answer: Variable pseudomyopia and near-point issues. Cycloplegic refraction.

40. A cycloplegic refraction is essential in a squinting child to reveal:

Show answer

Answer: Latent hypermetropia. Accommodative component.
