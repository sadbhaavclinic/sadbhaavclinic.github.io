Ophthalmic Nursing Mock Test 3: Nursing Assessment, Diagnostic Tests, Triage and Trauma (40 MCQs)

For: Optometrists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • 40 original questions on nursing assessment, diagnostic tests, triage and trauma.
  • Answer all, then press "Check my score": correct answers turn green, wrong ones red.
  • Open "Show answer" for the explanation.
  • Revise from the course modules.

Practise: Mock 1 · Mock 2 · Mock 3 · Mock 4 · Viva and OSCE · Career guide

Set a timer for 40 minutes. The option order is mixed, so read every choice.

1. Visual acuity should be tested:

Show answer

Answer: Each eye separately, with current glasses. Occlude with an occluder, not fingers.

2. A patient reads the 6/60 line at 3 m. The acuity is recorded as:

Show answer

Answer: 3/60. Record the actual test distance.

3. If vision improves with a pinhole, the cause is most likely:

Show answer

Answer: Refractive error. Pinhole reduces blur from refraction.

4. Near vision is commonly recorded using:

Show answer

Answer: N-series (N6, N8). At 33–40 cm.

5. Light perception with accurate projection means:

Show answer

Answer: Patient sees light and can tell its direction. Record PL+ with projection.

6. A tumbling E chart is useful for:

Show answer

Answer: People who cannot read letters. Also Landolt C.

7. The swinging torch test detects:

Show answer

Answer: Relative afferent pupil defect. Optic nerve or major retinal disease.

8. Before Goldmann applanation the nurse instils:

Show answer

Answer: Topical anaesthetic with fluorescein. Then cobalt blue light.

9. Rebound tonometry is especially useful for:

Show answer

Answer: Children and home monitoring. No anaesthetic needed.

10. Central corneal thickness is measured by:

Show answer

Answer: Pachymetry. Affects IOP readings.

11. Keratometry measures:

Show answer

Answer: Corneal curvature. Needed for IOL power.

12. A-scan or optical biometry measures:

Show answer

Answer: Axial length of the eye. IOL calculation.

13. B-scan ultrasound is most useful when:

Show answer

Answer: The view of the retina is blocked by dense cataract or haemorrhage. Detects detachment or tumour.

14. Gonioscopy examines the:

Show answer

Answer: Drainage angle. Uses a contact lens.

15. The most common side effect during IV fluorescein injection is:

Show answer

Answer: Nausea. Usually brief.

16. Which emergency drug must be ready during fluorescein angiography?

Show answer

Answer: Adrenaline. For anaphylaxis.

17. OCT produces images using:

Show answer

Answer: Light (low-coherence interferometry). Cross-sectional retina.

18. Schirmer test strips are placed:

Show answer

Answer: In the outer third of the lower lid. Read after 5 minutes.

19. An exophthalmometer is used in:

Show answer

Answer: Thyroid eye disease. Measures proptosis.

20. Ishihara plates are best viewed in:

Show answer

Answer: Good daylight. About 75 cm.

21. Which patient should be seen first?

Show answer

Answer: Chemical splash 10 minutes ago. Immediate irrigation.

22. A telephone caller reports flashes and a new shower of floaters since this morning. Advice:

Show answer

Answer: Same-day assessment. Possible retinal tear.

23. Irrigation of a chemical injury should use:

Show answer

Answer: Normal saline or Ringer's lactate (or clean water if nothing else). Never try to neutralise.

24. During irrigation for lime injury, particles are removed by:

Show answer

Answer: Everting lids and sweeping fornices with a moist cotton bud. Particles keep releasing alkali.

25. A patient injured while hammering metal has a small corneal wound and irregular pupil. Suspect:

Show answer

Answer: Intraocular foreign body with open globe. CT scan; shield.

26. Which is correct for an open globe injury?

Show answer

Answer: Rigid shield, nil by mouth, antiemetic, urgent surgeon. Avoid pressure.

27. Hyphaema patients are advised to avoid aspirin because:

Show answer

Answer: It increases the risk of rebleeding. Follow doctor's advice.

28. Patients with sickle cell disease and hyphaema are at higher risk of:

Show answer

Answer: Severe IOP rise and optic nerve damage. Sickled cells block outflow.

29. Double vision on looking up after a punch to the cheek suggests:

Show answer

Answer: Orbital floor (blow-out) fracture. Avoid nose-blowing.

30. A child with an orbital floor fracture, little bruising, vomiting and slow heart rate needs:

Show answer

Answer: Urgent surgical release (trapped muscle). White-eyed blow-out fracture.

31. Lid lacerations near the inner corner may involve the:

Show answer

Answer: Canaliculus (tear drainage). Needs specialist repair.

32. Arc eye after welding is treated with:

Show answer

Answer: Lubricants, pain relief, antibiotic ointment; heals in a day or two. Prevention: welding mask.

33. Orbital cellulitis in a child requires monitoring of:

Show answer

Answer: Vision, pupils, eye movements and colour vision. Optic nerve compromise.

34. Sudden painless loss of vision in a 70-year-old with scalp tenderness needs urgent:

Show answer

Answer: ESR/CRP and steroids as ordered. Giant cell arteritis.

35. The oculocardiac reflex causes:

Show answer

Answer: Bradycardia. Traction on eye muscles.

36. A diabetic patient fasting for surgery becomes sweaty and confused. First check:

Show answer

Answer: Blood glucose. Treat hypoglycaemia.

37. Adult intramuscular adrenaline dose in anaphylaxis is usually:

Show answer

Answer: 0.5 mg (0.5 mL of 1 mg/mL). Outer thigh.

38. Fireworks injuries in India peak around:

Show answer

Answer: Diwali and festival seasons. Prevention teaching.

39. A worker grinding metal without goggles has a speck on the cornea with a brown ring. This is:

Show answer

Answer: Rust ring from a metallic foreign body. Remove and treat.

40. In triage, which is most urgent?

Show answer

Answer: Pain and falling vision 3 days after cataract surgery. Suspect endophthalmitis.
