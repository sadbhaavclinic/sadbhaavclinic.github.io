OCT, Fundus Cameras and Ultra-Widefield Imaging Compared: Which Device for Which Clinic

For: PG residents · Practising ophthalmologists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • OCT answers "what is in the layers"; a fundus camera documents "what it looks like"; ultra-widefield (UWF) imaging shows "how much of the periphery is involved".
  • Swept-source OCT is generally faster than spectral-domain and sees deeper (choroid, lamina cribrosa) with its longer wavelength.
  • An Optos 200° single image covers about 82% of the retina but in pseudocolour; Clarus gives a true-colour 133° image, montaged to 200°.
  • Field-of-view numbers are not comparable between makers: Optos quotes internal angles, most others external.
  • For a general clinic, a good OCT with a fundus camera usually comes before UWF.

Imaging decides how quickly a clinic can diagnose and follow macular, retinal and glaucoma disease, and it is also the largest single equipment purchase after the operating theatre. This page compares the three main device families, what each does best, the main models, and how to choose.

The three device families

DeviceWhat it showsBest forLimits
OCT (spectral-domain or swept-source)Cross-sections and thickness maps of the retina, nerve fibre layer, disc and (with OCTA) the capillary layersMacular oedema, AMD, macular holes, glaucoma follow-up, anti-VEGF decisionsSmall field (about 6–12 mm per scan); needs clear media
Fundus camera (45–50°)Colour, red-free and autofluorescence photographs; FFA on angiography-capable modelsDocumentation, diabetic screening, disc photographs, FFAPosterior pole only unless montaged; non-mydriatic models need a reasonable pupil
Ultra-widefield (UWF)Single images of the far periphery in all four quadrants; UWF angiography on some modelsDiabetic retinopathy and vein occlusion (peripheral non-perfusion), uveitis, peripheral lesionsExpensive; lid and lash artefacts; colour differs from the real fundus on some systems

OCT: spectral-domain vs swept-source

Both are Fourier-domain OCT. Swept-source devices generally scan faster than spectral-domain devices and use a longer wavelength, which shows the choroid, lamina cribrosa and sclera better and reduces the signal roll-off seen in spectral-domain OCT.

  • Thickness numbers are not interchangeable between makers, because each device segments the layers differently. Follow a patient on the same machine, and note the device on every report.
  • OCT angiography shows flow without dye but has a small field and motion artefacts; it does not show leakage, so FFA still has a role.
  • Glaucoma analysis depends on the normative database; check that it includes enough Asian or Indian eyes for your patients.

Reading the scans is covered in OCT interpretation tips, OCT macula reference and OCT disc and glaucoma reference.

Fundus cameras

  • Tabletop mydriatic or non-mydriatic (45–50°): the workhorse for documentation and diabetic screening. Angiography-capable models also do FFA.
  • Confocal scanning cameras: work through smaller pupils with less scatter from cataract.
  • Hand-held cameras: cheaper and portable, for camps, wards and children; image quality depends heavily on the operator.
  • Smartphone adapters: the lowest-cost route to documentation and tele-consultation; useful, but not a substitute for a camera in a diabetic screening programme.

Ultra-widefield: Optos vs Clarus

Optos (California, Daytona, Silverstone)Zeiss Clarus (500, 700)
FieldSingle 200° image (internal angle) covering about 82% of the retinaSingle 133° image; two images montaged to 200°
CaptureUnder 0.4 s, through an undilated pupilUnder 0.2 s per image
ColourPseudocolour from red and green lasers; can differ from the real fundusTrue colour
ArtefactsLashes and lids often obscure part of the imageFewer lash artefacts; montage needs a cooperative patient
In studiesCaptured a larger area of fundus than a single Clarus imageDiabetic retinopathy grading broadly consistent with Optos

Reading field numbers: Optos measures from the centre of the eye (internal angle), which gives bigger numbers; Clarus and most lens specifications measure from the pupil (external angle). DRCR Retina Network defines UWF as at least 100° of fundus.

Which device for which clinic?

PracticeSensible first imaging
General ophthalmology, cataract-focusedSpectral-domain OCT with a fundus camera (combined units save space); refurbished is reasonable
Glaucoma-heavyOCT with a strong glaucoma package and normative database, plus perimetry
Medical retina and anti-VEGFOCT with eye tracking or reliable follow-up registration, OCTA, and FFA capability
Diabetic screening or outreachNon-mydriatic or hand-held fundus camera; UWF if the budget allows
Vitreoretinal referral centreUWF imaging with angiography, alongside OCT and OCTA

Indicative costs

  • International price surveys put a basic non-mydriatic fundus camera at about US$8,000–25,000, and premium OCT with OCTA at about US$70,000–110,000.
  • Indian listings show refurbished OCTs from about ₹10–13 lakh, new premium OCTs from about ₹33 lakh, tabletop fundus cameras about ₹5.5–8 lakh, and a premium UWF camera about ₹75 lakh.
  • OCTA, anterior segment modules and glaucoma software are often priced as add-ons: compare quotes like for like. More buying advice is in the eye clinic equipment buying guide.

Where residents slip

  • Comparing retinal thickness from two different OCT makes as if they were the same measurement.
  • Calling a pseudocolour Optos image "haemorrhage" or "naevus" by colour alone.
  • Trusting an OCT segmentation without scrolling through the B-scans.
  • Forgetting that a normal OCT does not rule out peripheral retinal disease.
