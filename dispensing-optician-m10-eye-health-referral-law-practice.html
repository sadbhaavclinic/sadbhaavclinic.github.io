Ophthalmic Dispensing Module 10: Eye Health for Opticians, Referral, Law, Ethics and Practice Management

For: Allied eye-care staff

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Opticians are often the first point of contact: know red flags and refer promptly.
  • Dispense only against a valid prescription where the law requires; never alter a prescription without the prescriber.
  • Honest advice on lens options and prices builds trust; avoid pressure selling.
  • Keep records of orders, measurements, verification and advice.

Exam map: Anatomy and common eye conditions, recognising referral signs, professional conduct, consumer law, communication and business.

Eye anatomy and common conditions (dispensing view)

  • Refractive errors and presbyopia (Module 2); cataract (glare, frequent Rx changes, myopic shift); glaucoma (silent; encourage eye tests over 40 and with family history); diabetic eye disease (fluctuating vision); AMD (distortion); keratoconus (rapid cylinder changes, poor acuity with glasses); dry eye (worse with screens).
  • Children: squint and amblyopia (glasses must be worn as prescribed), myopia progression (outdoor time, myopia control).

When to refer (red flags)

Symptom or signAction
Sudden loss or blurring of visionSame-day eye doctor
Flashes, new floaters, curtainSame day
Painful red eye, light sensitivitySame day
New double visionSame day / urgent
Distortion of straight linesWithin days (possible wet AMD)
Acuity not reaching expected level with the RxRefer for examination
Headache with visual disturbance, unequal pupils, drooping lidUrgent medical review
Child with white pupil or new squintUrgent

Law and professional conduct

  • Regulation varies: UK dispensing opticians register with the General Optical Council (GOC) after an approved qualification (e.g. FBDO via ABDO); USA: licensure in many (not all) states, with ABO and NCLE certification; Australia: no national registration for dispensing opticians (Certificate IV in Optical Dispensing); India: dispensing is largely unregulated, with optometry recognised under the National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions Act, 2021.
  • Restrictions: some countries restrict sales to children, to people with low vision, and of contact lenses to registered professionals; know local rules.
  • Consumer law: clear prices, honest descriptions (e.g. no false claims for blue-filter lenses), warranties, refunds as required (India: Consumer Protection Act, 2019).
  • Records: prescription details, measurements, order, verification, collection notes, complaints.
  • Confidentiality and data protection: patient records and prescriptions are personal data (India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023).

Communication and ethics

  • Find out needs (work, hobbies, screen time, driving); explain options in plain language with pros, cons and price; let the patient choose.
  • Handle complaints calmly: listen, check, solve, document.
  • Do not overstate benefits (“cures eye strain”, “protects from all disease”).
  • Accessibility: large-print paperwork, good lighting, patience with older patients.

Practice management basics

  • Stock control of frames and lenses, supplier relations, lab turnaround, remakes and error tracking.
  • Pricing transparency, packages, insurance where relevant.
  • Staff training, quality checks, customer feedback.

Practice questions (short answer and viva)

Name four red flags that need same-day referral.

Sudden vision loss, flashes and curtain, painful red eye, new double vision.

A customer’s acuity with the new Rx is only 6/12. What do you do?

Check the dispensing; if correct, refer for eye examination.

Which body registers dispensing opticians in the UK?

The General Optical Council (GOC).

What should you avoid when selling blue-filter lenses?

Claims of proven benefit for eye strain, sleep or eye disease.

Why keep verification records?

Quality assurance, complaint handling and legal protection.

Answer: Nuclear cataract. Index myopia.

3. Distortion of straight lines should be referred:

Show answer

Answer: Within days (possible wet AMD). Treatable if early.

4. FBDO is a qualification of the:

Show answer

Answer: ABDO (UK). Association of British Dispensing Opticians.

5. In India, consumer complaints are governed by the:

Show answer

Answer: Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Consumer rights.

6. An optician should alter a prescription:

Show answer

Answer: Only with the prescriber's agreement. Professional conduct.

7. A child's new squint should be:

Show answer

Answer: Referred urgently. Exclude serious causes.

8. Good communication when presenting lens options includes:

Show answer

Answer: Explaining benefits, limits and prices and letting the patient choose. Ethics.
