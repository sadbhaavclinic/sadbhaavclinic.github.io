Last updated: October 2026

LevelDescription

BSupportive evidence from well-conducted cohort studies (prospective cohort studies, registries, or meta-analyses of cohort studies), or from a well-conducted case-control study

Diabetic retinopathy: International (ICDR) classification

GradeFindings
No apparent retinopathyNo abnormalities
Mild NPDRMicroaneurysms only
Moderate NPDRMore than microaneurysms but less than severe NPDR
Severe NPDRAny one of the 4-2-1 rule (see below), and no PDR
PDRNeovascularisation (NVD/NVE) and/or vitreous or preretinal haemorrhage

4-2-1 rule: severe intraretinal haemorrhages (≥ 20) in 4 quadrants, definite venous beading in 2 quadrants, or prominent IRMA in 1 quadrant. Two or more features = very severe NPDR.

More: Diabetic retinopathy guide

PDR: high-risk characteristics (Diabetic Retinopathy Study)

Any one of:
NVD ≥ one-quarter to one-third of a disc area, with or without vitreous/preretinal haemorrhage
NVD smaller than this, with vitreous or preretinal haemorrhage
NVE ≥ one-half disc area, with vitreous or preretinal haemorrhage

Hypertensive retinopathy: Keith-Wagener-Barker (1939)

The first system to correlate retinal findings with the severity and prognosis of hypertension.

GradeFindings
1Mild generalised arteriolar narrowing
2More marked narrowing with focal constriction and arteriovenous nicking
3Grade 2 plus flame haemorrhages, cotton-wool spots and hard exudates
4Grade 3 plus optic disc swelling

Main limitation: grades 1 and 2 are hard to tell apart, which led to newer classifications.

Papilloedema: clinical stages

StageFeatures
EarlyDisc hyperaemia, mild swelling, loss of spontaneous venous pulsation

AtrophicSecondary optic atrophy with gliosis and narrowed vessels

Relative afferent pupillary defect: clinical grading

GradeResponse when the light swings to the affected eye
IWeak initial constriction followed by greater redilation
IIInitial stall, then definite dilation
IIIImmediate dilation
IVImmediate dilation after prolonged (about 6 s) illumination of the other eye
VImmediate dilation with no constriction at all

Clinical grades are affected by pupil size and vary between observers. Neutral density filters (0.3 log unit steps) quantify the RAPD more accurately.

Laser safety classes (IEC 60825-1)

ClassHazard
1 / 1MSafe under reasonably foreseeable conditions (1M: unless viewed with optical instruments)
2 / 2MVisible lasers up to 1 mW; protected by the blink reflex for brief exposure
