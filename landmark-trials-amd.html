Landmark Trials in AMD: TAP to Faricimab, AREDS and Geographic Atrophy

For: PG residents

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • TAP: Fewer treated eyes lost 15 letters or more (61% vs 46% kept vision overall).
  • VIP: No significant benefit at 1 year; a modest benefit appeared at 2 years.
  • VISION: Pegaptanib 0.3 mg slowed loss: 70% vs 55% lost fewer than 15 letters.
  • MARINA: About 95% of treated eyes lost fewer than 15 letters vs 62% with sham, and about a third gained 15 letters or more.
  • ANCHOR: About 96% vs 64% lost fewer than 15 letters; about 40% vs 6% gained 15 letters or more at 1 year.
  • PIER: Vision improved during loading, then drifted back towards baseline with quarterly dosing.

Wet AMD treatment changed twice in a decade, first with photodynamic therapy and then with anti-VEGF injections. Since then the trials have mostly asked how often to inject. Dry AMD trials ask a different question: who benefits from supplements, and what the new complement inhibitors can and cannot do.

How each trial is set out: design and size, the question it asked, what it found, why it matters, the trap examiners like to set, and where it stands today. Numbers are rounded; check the original paper before quoting a figure in a thesis.

Wet AMD: before anti-VEGF

TAP (1999)

Treatment of AMD with Photodynamic Therapy

DesignVerteporfin PDT vs placebo · 609 patients · subfoveal CNV with a classic component · 1 year (extended to 2)
QuestionCan photodynamic therapy prevent vision loss without burning the fovea?
FindingFewer treated eyes lost 15 letters or more (61% vs 46% kept vision overall). The benefit was clear only when the lesion was predominantly classic (67% vs 39%).
Why it mattersThe first treatment for subfoveal CNV that spared the fovea; lesion type on fluorescein angiography decided who was treated.
Viva trapThe predominantly classic benefit came from a subgroup; minimally classic lesions did not benefit.
TodayNot used alone for typical AMD; still used with anti-VEGF in polypoidal CNV, and at half dose in chronic CSCR.

VIP (2001)

Verteporfin in Photodynamic Therapy

DesignVerteporfin PDT vs placebo · 339 patients, mostly occult CNV with no classic component · 2 years
QuestionDoes PDT help occult CNV, which TAP did not study?
FindingNo significant benefit at 1 year; a modest benefit appeared at 2 years.
Why it mattersBriefly extended PDT to small occult lesions with recent progression.
Viva trapAcute severe vision loss within a week of treatment occurred in a few per cent of treated eyes.
TodayObsolete.

VISION (2004)

VEGF Inhibition Study in Ocular Neovascularization

DesignPegaptanib (0.3, 1 or 3 mg) vs sham every 6 weeks · 1,186 patients · all lesion types · 1 year
QuestionDoes blocking VEGF inside the eye help wet AMD?
FindingPegaptanib 0.3 mg slowed loss: 70% vs 55% lost fewer than 15 letters. Mean vision still fell.
Why it mattersProof of concept for intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy.
Viva trapPegaptanib slowed vision loss; it did not improve vision. Ranibizumab was the first drug to improve vision on average.
TodayObsolete: pegaptanib blocks only the VEGF165 isoform.

Wet AMD: anti-VEGF arrives

MARINA (2006)

Minimally Classic/Occult Trial of the Anti-VEGF Antibody Ranibizumab

DesignMonthly ranibizumab 0.3 or 0.5 mg vs sham · 716 patients · minimally classic or occult CNV · 2 years
QuestionDoes monthly ranibizumab work in lesions that PDT could not treat?
FindingAbout 95% of treated eyes lost fewer than 15 letters vs 62% with sham, and about a third gained 15 letters or more. Mean gain about +7 letters vs a loss of about 10 with sham.
Why it mattersWith ANCHOR, made anti-VEGF the standard for every lesion type.
Viva trapThe gains needed monthly injections for 2 years; less frequent dosing in later trials gave smaller gains.

ANCHOR (2006)

Anti-VEGF Antibody for the Treatment of Predominantly Classic CNV in AMD

DesignMonthly ranibizumab vs verteporfin PDT · 423 patients · predominantly classic CNV · 2 years
QuestionIs ranibizumab better than the then standard, PDT?
FindingAbout 96% vs 64% lost fewer than 15 letters; about 40% vs 6% gained 15 letters or more at 1 year.
Why it mattersReplaced PDT for classic CNV.
Viva trapThe comparator was an active treatment (PDT), not sham, unlike MARINA.

Wet AMD: how often to inject

PIER (2008)

Phase IIIb, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Masked, Sham Injection-Controlled Study of Ranibizumab

DesignRanibizumab monthly for 3 doses then every 3 months vs sham · 184 patients · 1 year
QuestionCan injections be given quarterly after loading?
FindingVision improved during loading, then drifted back towards baseline with quarterly dosing.
Why it mattersShowed that fixed quarterly dosing is too infrequent for most patients.
Viva trapRemember PIER as the trial that failed quarterly dosing, which led to individualised regimens.

PRONTO (2007–2009)

Prospective OCT Imaging of Patients with Neovascular AMD Treated with Intraocular Lucentis

DesignRanibizumab × 3, then OCT-guided as-needed retreatment · 40 patients · open-label, no control · 2 years
QuestionCan OCT guide retreatment so fewer injections are needed?
FindingVision gains similar to MARINA and ANCHOR (about +9 to +11 letters) with roughly half the injections.
Why it mattersIntroduced OCT-guided as-needed (PRN) dosing.
Viva trapA small uncontrolled study: it showed feasibility, not equivalence to monthly dosing.

SUSTAIN (2011)

Study of Ranibizumab in Patients with Subfoveal CNV Secondary to AMD

DesignRanibizumab × 3 then PRN · 513 patients · single-arm · 1 year
QuestionDoes PRN dosing after loading work outside a small study?
FindingThe early gain peaked at about +6 letters and settled at about +4 letters by 12 months.
Why it mattersShowed that infrequent PRN retreatment lets early gains erode.
Viva trapSingle-arm, not randomised.

CATT (2011–2012)

Comparison of AMD Treatments Trials

DesignRanibizumab vs bevacizumab, each monthly or PRN · 1,208 patients · USA · 2 years
QuestionIs cheap bevacizumab as good as ranibizumab, and is PRN as good as monthly?
FindingBevacizumab was equivalent to ranibizumab on the same schedule. PRN gave slightly less gain than monthly (about 2 letters at 2 years).
Why it mattersMade off-label bevacizumab a legitimate first choice worldwide.
Viva trapSerious systemic adverse events were slightly more frequent with bevacizumab, but not with a consistent pattern; the trial was not powered for safety.

IVAN (2012–2013)

Inhibit VEGF in Age-related Choroidal Neovascularisation

DesignRanibizumab vs bevacizumab, monthly or discontinuous · 610 patients · UK · 2 years
QuestionDoes the CATT result hold in the UK?
FindingBevacizumab was non-inferior to ranibizumab, and discontinuous dosing was non-inferior to continuous dosing for vision.
Why it mattersSupported bevacizumab in publicly funded health systems.
Viva trapIVAN and CATT agree on drug equivalence; both found a small edge for continuous dosing.

HARBOR (2013)

Ranibizumab 0.5 mg vs 2.0 mg, monthly vs PRN

DesignRanibizumab 0.5 or 2.0 mg, monthly or PRN · 1,098 patients · 2 years
QuestionDoes a higher dose of ranibizumab work better?
FindingNo: the 2.0 mg dose was not better than 0.5 mg.
Why it mattersKept 0.5 mg as the standard dose.
Viva trapA higher dose does not mean a longer interval for ranibizumab; longer intervals came from newer molecules.

Wet AMD: longer intervals

VIEW 1 and 2 (2012)

VEGF Trap-Eye: Investigation of Efficacy and Safety in Wet AMD

DesignAflibercept 2 mg every 4 or 8 weeks (after 3 loading doses), or 0.5 mg every 4 weeks, vs monthly ranibizumab · 2,419 patients · 1 year
QuestionCan aflibercept every 8 weeks match monthly ranibizumab?
FindingYes: about 95% of eyes in every arm kept vision, with similar letter gains.
Why it mattersAflibercept every 8 weeks became a standard, halving clinic visits.
Viva trapNon-inferiority, not superiority: aflibercept was "as good as", with fewer injections.

HAWK and HARRIER (2020)

Brolucizumab vs aflibercept in neovascular AMD

DesignBrolucizumab every 12 or 8 weeks vs aflibercept every 8 weeks · 1,817 patients · 2 years
QuestionCan a smaller, longer-acting molecule extend the interval?
FindingNon-inferior vision, with more than half of brolucizumab eyes on 12-weekly dosing and drier OCT scans.
Why it mattersApproved, but its use fell after reports of intraocular inflammation and occlusive retinal vasculitis.
Viva trapRemember brolucizumab for retinal vasculitis: a post-marketing safety signal not obvious in the trials.

TENAYA and LUCERNE (2022)

Faricimab vs aflibercept in neovascular AMD

DesignFaricimab up to every 16 weeks vs aflibercept every 8 weeks · 1,329 patients · 2 years
QuestionDoes blocking angiopoietin-2 as well as VEGF allow longer intervals?
FindingFaricimab was non-inferior, with about 45% of patients on 16-weekly dosing at year 1.
Why it mattersFaricimab became the first bispecific intravitreal antibody approved for wet AMD.
Viva trapThe aflibercept arm was fixed at 8 weeks, so the trials compare a flexible regimen with a fixed one.
TodayHigh-dose aflibercept 8 mg (PULSAR) also allows intervals of 12–16 weeks.

Polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy

EVEREST II (2017)

Ranibizumab with or without verteporfin PDT in PCV

DesignRanibizumab plus PDT vs ranibizumab alone · 322 Asian patients · 1 year (extended to 2)
QuestionDoes adding PDT to ranibizumab help polypoidal CNV?
FindingCombination therapy gave better vision (about +9 vs +6 letters) and more complete polyp regression (about 69% vs 34%), with fewer injections.
Why it mattersCombination therapy became the standard for PCV in many Asian centres.
Viva trapThe earlier EVEREST (2012) was small and anatomical; EVEREST II added a visual endpoint.

Dry AMD

AREDS (2001)

Age-Related Eye Disease Study

DesignAntioxidants (vitamins C and E, beta-carotene) and/or zinc vs placebo · 3,640 participants · mean 6.3 years
QuestionDo supplements slow progression to advanced AMD?
FindingIn intermediate AMD, or advanced AMD in one eye, the full formula cut progression to advanced AMD by about 25% and moderate vision loss by about 19%.
Why it mattersCreated the "AREDS formula" for patients at high risk.
Viva trapNo benefit in early AMD or in people without AMD, and no effect on cataract.

AREDS2 (2013)

Age-Related Eye Disease Study 2

DesignLutein and zeaxanthin and/or omega-3 added to AREDS formula variants · 4,203 participants · 5 years
QuestionCan the formula be improved, and beta-carotene removed?
FindingOmega-3 fatty acids added nothing. Lutein and zeaxanthin were a safe replacement for beta-carotene, which raised lung cancer risk in former smokers.
Why it mattersThe current AREDS2 formula: vitamins C and E, zinc, copper, lutein and zeaxanthin.
Viva trapBeta-carotene was removed because of lung cancer risk in smokers and former smokers, not because it did not work.

OAKS and DERBY (2023)

Pegcetacoplan for geographic atrophy

DesignIntravitreal pegcetacoplan (complement C3 inhibitor) monthly or every other month vs sham · 1,258 patients · 2 years
QuestionCan complement inhibition slow geographic atrophy?
FindingLesion growth slowed by roughly 15–20% at 24 months (OAKS met its 12-month endpoint; DERBY did not). Vision did not differ.
Why it mattersThe first approved treatment for geographic atrophy (USA, 2023).
Viva trapThe benefit is on lesion growth, not vision. New wet AMD was more frequent with monthly treatment, and rare occlusive vasculitis has been reported.
TodayNot approved in Europe or the UK, where regulators judged the benefit not clinically meaningful.

GATHER2 (2023)

Avacincaptad pegol for geographic atrophy

DesignAvacincaptad pegol (C5 inhibitor) monthly vs sham · 448 patients · 1 year
QuestionDoes C5 inhibition slow geographic atrophy?
FindingLesion growth was about 14–18% slower at 12 months; vision was unchanged.
Why it mattersThe second approved complement inhibitor for geographic atrophy (USA, 2023).
Viva trapLike pegcetacoplan: anatomy improved, function did not, and choroidal neovascularisation was more common.

Landmark trials by subspecialty: AMD · Retinal vein occlusion · Diabetic retinopathy and DME · Vitreoretinal and macular · Glaucoma · Cornea and cataract · Uveitis and oncology · Paediatrics and strabismus · Neuro-ophthalmology. One-line revision table: all landmark trials.
