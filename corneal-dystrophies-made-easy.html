Corneal Dystrophies Made Easy: Layers, Stains and Genes

Last updated: October 2026

There are more than 20 corneal dystrophies, but you only need to know which layer each starts in and a few "odd ones out". The layer predicts the symptoms, the look at the slit lamp, the stain and the operation.

Four rules

1. The layer decides the symptomEpithelium and Bowman: recurrent erosions and pain, often in childhood. Stroma: slowly falling vision and glare. Endothelium: oedema, worse in the morning.
2. The layer decides the operationSuperficial (epithelium, Bowman, anterior stroma): PTK or superficial keratectomy. Stroma with healthy endothelium: DALK. Endothelium: DMEK or DSAEK.
3. Almost everything is autosomal dominantThe exceptions are macular and gelatinous drop-like (recessive), CHED (recessive) and X-linked endothelial dystrophy. Note: Lisch dystrophy, once called X-linked, is now classed as dominant (IC3D 2024).
4. One gene builds a whole familyTGFBI (5q31) causes Reis–Bücklers, Thiel–Behnke, granular 1 and 2, and lattice 1. The amino acid hotspots R124 and R555 decide which one.

Memory hook: Marilyn Monroe Always Gets Her Man in L.A. City: Macular – Mucopolysaccharide – Alcian blue; Granular – Hyaline – Masson; Lattice – Amyloid – Congo red.

Memory hook: Macular is the odd one out in everything: recessive, not TGFBI, whole stroma to the limbus, cloudy in between, thin cornea, earliest loss of vision.

Epithelial dystrophies

DystrophyKey features
Epithelial basement membrane (EBMD, map-dot-fingerprint, Cogan)Most common; usually degenerative rather than inherited; maps, dots and fingerprint lines; recurrent erosions; irregular astigmatism that spoils keratometry before cataract surgery
MeesmannMyriad tiny intraepithelial cysts in childhood; keratin genes KRT3 and KRT12; usually mild
LischWhorled grey band-shaped epithelium seen best on retroillumination; MCOLN1; now autosomal dominant (IC3D 2024)
Epithelial recurrent erosion (ERED)Erosions from childhood, often improve with age; COL17A1; Franceschetti, Smolandiensis and Helsinglandica are now grouped with it
Gelatinous drop-likeMulberry-like subepithelial amyloid; recessive (TACSTD2); common in Japan; recurs after surgery

Other stromal dystrophies

  • Schnyder: central ring or disc of cholesterol crystals (only in about half), dense arcus, mid-peripheral haze; stains with Oil red O; UBIAD1. Check fasting lipids: many patients have hyperlipidaemia.
  • Congenital stromal: flaky cloudy stroma at birth (DCN, decorin). Fleck: tiny asymptomatic flecks throughout (PIKFYVE).
  • Posterior amorphous, central cloudy of François, pre-Descemet: mostly asymptomatic posterior haze; PPPCD (punctiform and polychromatic pre-Descemet) is linked to PRDX3.

DystrophyOnsetKey featuresGene
FuchsUsually after 40; women more oftenCentral guttae → stromal oedema → epithelial bullae; morning blur. Guttae + oedema → DMEK or DSAEKTCF4 CTG repeat expansion (late onset); COL8A2 (early onset)

X-linked endothelialBirth (males)Moon-crater endothelial changes; clouding in malesX-linked
  • Cloudy cornea in a newborn: remember STUMPED: Sclerocornea, Tears in Descemet (birth trauma, congenital glaucoma), Ulcer, Metabolic (mucopolysaccharidoses), Peters anomaly, Endothelial dystrophy (CHED), Dermoid.
  • PPCD vs ICE syndrome: both have epithelial-like endothelium. PPCD is bilateral, inherited and found in the young; ICE is unilateral, sporadic, in middle-aged women.

Treatment by layer

ProblemOptions
Recurrent erosions (EBMD, ERED, Reis–Bücklers, lattice)Lubricants, bandage contact lens, hypertonic saline; debridement with diamond burr polishing, anterior stromal puncture, or PTK
Anterior stromal deposits (granular, Avellino, Reis–Bücklers, Thiel–Behnke)PTK first; DALK if deeper; recurrences can be re-treated with PTK
Deeper stromal disease (lattice, macular, Schnyder)DALK (macular may also affect the endothelium, so PK is sometimes needed)
Endothelial disease (Fuchs, CHED, PPCD)DMEK or DSAEK; Descemet stripping only (DSO) with a ROCK inhibitor for selected Fuchs patients

Where residents slip

  • Mixing up the stains: use the Marilyn Monroe line.
  • Calling macular dystrophy dominant: it is recessive and not a TGFBI disease.
  • Doing LASIK on a patient with faint anterior stromal dots: Avellino dystrophy can explode after LASIK.
  • Confusing CHED with congenital glaucoma: CHED has normal IOP and a normal-sized cornea, with no Haab striae.
  • Forgetting EBMD before cataract surgery: irregular topography gives wrong K readings and toric IOL errors. Treat the surface first.
