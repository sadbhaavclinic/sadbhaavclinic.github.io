Eye Drop Chart Maker: Printable Day-by-Day Tapering Schedule After Eye Surgery (English, Hindi, Gujarati)

For: PG residents · Practising ophthalmologists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Choose the surgery or condition to load usual drops and tapers, then edit them to match your prescription.
  • Each day gets a row with one tick box per drop, so patients can see what is due and what they missed.
  • Instructions print in English, Hindi or Gujarati; drop names stay as on the bottles.
  • Charts longer than 2 weeks print on extra pages.
  • Nothing about the patient is saved or uploaded.

Patients go home with three or four bottles and a taper that changes every week. Missed doses, stopping steroids too early and using the wrong drop are common reasons for inflammation and repeat visits. A chart with one tick box per drop, day by day, makes the plan easy to follow.

Part of the clinic print tools: fill in, preview and print on your letterhead. Nothing you type about a patient is uploaded or saved.

Choose the surgery or condition, check the drops and the taper, and print a day-by-day chart the patient ticks after each drop. Nothing is uploaded or saved except your clinic details (if you choose).

Patient and treatment
Drops and taper

Taper: write each stage as times a day × days, separated by commas. Example: 6x7, 4x7, 2x7, 1x7 means 6 times a day for 7 days, then 4 times a day for 7 days, and so on.

Medicine (name and strength)TypeTaperEye

Good practice

  • Go through the chart with the patient or the person who will put the drops in, and point to each bottle.
  • Write the drop names exactly as on the bottles; the chart keeps names in English so they match.
  • Shake suspensions such as prednisolone acetate before each use.
  • Ask the patient to bring the chart to each visit; gaps show you where the plan went wrong.
  • For glaucoma patients, add their usual glaucoma drops so they are not stopped by mistake.
