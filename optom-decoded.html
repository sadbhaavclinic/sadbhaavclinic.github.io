OptomDecoded: Optometry Licensing Exam Question Bank with 5,885 MCQs, 150 OSCE Stations and 800 Viva Questions

For: Optometrists

Last updated: November 2026

OptomDecoded: Comprehensive Optometry is a licensing exam question bank for optometrists, written by Dr Dhaval Patel, MD (AIIMS). In one volume it holds 5,885 original MCQs (5,255 topic questions in 14 chapters and 6 full mock tests of 105 questions), 150 OSCE stations and 800 viva questions, all with explanations.

What makes it different

  • Written and practical stages in one book. Topic MCQs and mock tests for the written papers, OSCE stations built around a patient, a set of findings or a calculation, and viva questions with a model answer and a likely follow-up.
  • Every option explained. Each MCQ has the answer, the reasoning (with the working shown for every calculation), a "why not" for each wrong option, and a one-line pearl.
  • Key Points before every part, a short summary of what examiners expect you to know.
  • Calculation drills: a whole chapter of vergence, effectivity, Prentice's rule, tear lens, magnification and statistics problems, where marks are most often lost.
  • Referral and scope flags. Explanations say when the optometrist's job is to refer, and how urgently, and note where drug use and prescribing differ between countries.
  • International references: WHO, the World Council of Optometry competency framework and ISO standards, in British spelling with SI units.
  • Tagged by type and difficulty (recall, concept, case, data; D1 to D3), and answer letters spread evenly so there is no pattern to guess.

  • Optometrists sitting a licensing or registration examination at home or in a new country: NBEO, OCANZ, OEBC, GOC routes, the Gulf and others.

  • Practising optometrists who want a structured refresh of the whole syllabus.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I buy it? It is a Kindle book on Amazon. Use the buttons above, or search "OptomDecoded" on your own Amazon store. You can read it on a Kindle or in the free Kindle app on a phone, tablet or computer.

Are the questions from past papers? No. Every question, OSCE station and viva question is original, written to test the same knowledge in the same single-best-answer style.

Does it follow one country's rules? It is written to international references. Where practice differs, for example in the use and prescribing of drugs by optometrists, the explanation says so.

How do I report an error? Email drdpatel87@gmail.com. Corrections are welcome for future editions.
