Ophthalmic Technician Practical and Viva Questions: Vision, Refraction, Tonometry, Biometry, Fields, Drugs and OT

For: Optometrists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Practical exams test what you do, how safely you do it and how you treat the patient.
  • Tap a question to open the model answer.
  • Practise each station on real equipment under supervision.

These are the questions examiners and interviewers ask most often. Read the answer, then practise saying it aloud in your own words.

Visual acuity

How do you record vision of a patient who cannot read letters?

Use the E chart or Landolt C, picture charts for children; if below 1/60, record counting fingers at a distance, hand movements, perception of light with projection.

What does 6/18 mean?

The patient sees at 6 metres what a normal eye sees at 18 metres.

Why test with pinhole?

If vision improves with pinhole, a refractive error is likely; if not, look for other causes such as cataract, macular or optic nerve disease.

Refraction and lenses

How do you neutralise a lens by hand?

Move a lens over a cross: against motion means a plus lens, with motion a minus lens; neutralise with trial lenses until no motion. Rotate to detect a cylinder.

What is the use of the Jackson cross cylinder?

To refine the axis and power of the cylinder during subjective refraction.

What is the PD and how do you measure it?

Distance between the centres of the pupils, measured with a PD ruler or pupillometer, for distance and near.

Tonometry

Describe Goldmann applanation tonometry.

Anaesthetic and fluorescein drop, cobalt blue light, prism at 0° (or aligned for astigmatism), adjust until the inner edges of the two semicircles just touch; reading in mmHg = dial × 10.

How do you disinfect the tonometer prism?

Follow the manufacturer: commonly wiping and soaking in an approved disinfectant (for example dilute sodium hypochlorite or hydrogen peroxide) followed by thorough rinsing and drying, or use disposable prisms.

When is NCT reading unreliable?

Corneal scarring or oedema, irregular cornea, poor fixation, squeezing; confirm high or unexpected readings with Goldmann.

Biometry

What do you need for IOL power calculation?

Axial length, keratometry (K1, K2), often anterior chamber depth, lens thickness and white-to-white with optical biometers, the IOL A-constant and the formula.

When do you repeat biometry?

Axial length under 22 mm or over 25 mm, a difference over 0.3 mm between eyes, K difference over 1 D between eyes, or values that do not fit the refraction.

Immersion or contact A-scan: which is better?

Immersion, because contact technique can compress the cornea and give a falsely short axial length.

Perimetry and OCT

What are the reliability indices on a Humphrey field?

Fixation losses, false positives and false negatives; high values make the test unreliable.

How do you set up a patient for perimetry?

Correct near correction for the test distance, occlude the other eye, comfortable chin and forehead position, explain the test, monitor fixation.

Name common OCT artefacts.

Poor signal strength, decentration, segmentation errors, blink and motion artefacts, media opacity.

Drugs and dilation

Which drops are used for dilation?

Tropicamide 1% (often with phenylephrine 2.5%); cyclopentolate or atropine for cycloplegic refraction in children.

What precautions do you take before dilating?

Check the anterior chamber depth or angle as instructed, allergy history, ask about driving, avoid strong phenylephrine in infants and in severe heart disease.

Name the AAO cap colours.

Red: mydriatics and cycloplegics; pink: steroids; yellow: beta-blockers; teal: prostaglandins; purple: alpha-agonists; orange: carbonic anhydrase inhibitors; grey: NSAIDs; tan: anti-infectives.

Sterilisation and OT

What is TASS and how is it prevented?

Toxic anterior segment syndrome: sterile inflammation within 12–48 hours after surgery from toxic substances; prevent by proper rinsing of instruments, correct drug dilutions and avoiding detergent residue.

What is the Bowie-Dick test?

A daily test of air removal and steam penetration in pre-vacuum autoclaves.

What is the purpose of the time-out?

To confirm the right patient, procedure, eye and implant before the incision.

How a practical station is marked

  • Introduces self, confirms identity, explains the test and gains consent.
  • Hand hygiene and instrument hygiene.
  • Correct set-up and technique.
  • Correct reading, recording and interpretation.
  • Recognises red flags and informs the doctor.
  • Thanks the patient and tidies the station.
