Questions to Ask Before Cataract or LASIK Surgery: A Checklist for Any Surgeon

For: Patients and families

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • A good surgeon welcomes questions. Print this list, tick the ones that matter to you and take it to your consultation.
  • For cataract surgery, the biggest choices are the type of lens implant and what glasses you will still need.
  • For laser vision correction, the key question is whether your eyes are suitable, and which procedure is safest for you.
  • Ask for the total cost in writing and who to contact if there is a problem after hours.

Eye surgery is common and usually very successful, but it is still your decision. Asking the right questions helps you understand your options and set realistic expectations. These questions are useful with any surgeon, in any country.

Before cataract surgery

About your eye

  • How much is the cataract affecting my vision, and is now the right time to operate?
  • Do I have any other eye condition (glaucoma, macular degeneration, diabetic changes, astigmatism, dry eye) that may affect the result?
  • What vision can I realistically expect after surgery?

About the lens implant (IOL)

  • Which lens do you recommend for me: monofocal, toric (for astigmatism), extended depth of focus (EDOF) or multifocal? Why?
  • Will I need glasses afterwards for distance, computer or reading?
  • Should one eye be set for distance and the other for near (monovision)? Can I try this first with contact lenses?
  • What are the possible side effects of this lens, such as glare or haloes at night?

About the operation

  • Which technique will you use (phaco, manual small incision, laser-assisted) and why?
  • What anaesthesia will I have? Will I be awake?
  • How many such operations do you do, and what are your complication rates?
  • Should I stop any medicines before surgery (for example blood thinners)? Do my prostate medicines matter?
  • When will the second eye be done, or can both be done on the same day?

About recovery, cost and follow-up

  • How many follow-up visits will I need, and when can I drive, work, bathe and travel?
  • What is the total cost, including the lens, drops, tests and follow-up visits? Is it covered by my insurance (cashless or reimbursement)?
  • What will happen if there is a complication, and are further treatments included in the cost?
  • Whom do I call at night or at weekends if I have pain or reduced vision?

Before LASIK, SMILE, PRK or ICL

  • Is my glasses number stable, and am I old enough for surgery?
  • Are my corneas suitable? What did the corneal topography and thickness tests show? Is there any sign of keratoconus?
  • Which procedure do you recommend for me (LASIK, SMILE, PRK or an implantable contact lens) and why not the others?
  • What results can I realistically expect? Will I still need glasses for reading after 40?
  • What are the risks for me: dry eye, night glare or haloes, under- or over-correction, infection, corneal weakening (ectasia)?
  • How long until I can return to work, screens, driving, swimming and sport?
  • If I need a touch-up (enhancement) later, is it included in the cost?
  • Will this surgery affect future cataract surgery or glaucoma checks? Will I receive a record of my pre-surgery measurements? (Keep it safe; it helps choose the lens power for cataract surgery later.)
  • What is the total cost, and what happens if I am found unsuitable on the day?

Tips for your consultation

  • Bring your glasses, contact lens details, a list of your medicines and previous eye reports.
  • Stop wearing soft contact lenses for about 1–2 weeks (rigid lenses longer) before measurements for refractive or cataract surgery, as advised.
  • Bring a family member to help remember the information.
  • Ask for written information and the quotation in writing.
  • It is reasonable to take time to decide, and to seek a second opinion for any major decision.
