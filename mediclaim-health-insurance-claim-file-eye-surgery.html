Mediclaim Files for Eye Surgery: Documents, Bill Break-up and Case-by-Case Checklist for Cashless and Reimbursement Claims (India)

For: PG residents · Practising ophthalmologists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Use the free generator on this page to print a matching claim file on your letterhead.
  • Cataract surgery and intravitreal injections are day-care claims: no 24-hour admission is needed.
  • A complete file: consultation note, reports, operative summary, implant certificate with IOL sticker and invoice, discharge summary with ICD-10, itemised bill, receipt, pharmacy bills, claim form.
  • Check the policy's cataract sub-limit, premium-IOL rules and waiting period at counselling, and explain the patient's share in writing.
  • Bill honestly and in detail; never alter dates or inflate items.
Print tool: Surgery estimate and lens choice (show the patient's share before surgery).

Most patients who come for cataract or retina surgery have health insurance ("mediclaim"). Whether they claim cashless or pay first and claim reimbursement, the insurer judges the case from the papers you give. A clean, consistent file gets paid; a file with a wrong date, a missing lens sticker or a vague diagnosis gets queried, delayed or cut. This page shows new practitioners what to prepare, in what order, for common eye cases.

The short answer

  1. Cataract surgery and intravitreal injections are day-care procedures: 24 hours of admission is not needed.
  2. A standard file has: first consultation note, investigation reports, operative summary, implant certificate with IOL invoice and sticker, discharge summary with ICD-10 code, itemised final bill, payment receipt, prescriptions and pharmacy bills, and the hospital part of the claim form.
  3. Every page must show the same patient name, age, eye, dates and UHID, with the doctor's signature, registration number and clinic stamp.
  4. Use the right ICD-10 code (for age-related cataract, H25; other cataract, H26) and write the eye in full.
  5. Bill honestly and in detail. Never change dates, inflate items or split one procedure into several.

The standard claim file, in order

  1. Index sheet listing the documents (helps the claims officer and reduces queries).
  2. First consultation letter: date, complaint and its duration, findings (vision with glasses and pinhole, IOP, slit-lamp and fundus findings), diagnosis, and the advice for surgery.
  3. Investigation reports: biometry (A-scan or optical), keratometry; OCT, B-scan or specular microscopy if done; blood sugar or other tests if done. Each signed, with date.
  4. Pre-operative checklist and consent (keep in your record; send if asked). See informed consent.
  5. Operative summary: date and time, eye, procedure, anaesthesia, IOL model and power, any complication, surgeon's name.
  6. Implant certificate with the IOL sticker (lot and serial number) pasted on it, plus the IOL purchase invoice copy; some insurers also want the box.
  7. Discharge summary: name, age, sex, UHID or IPD number, address, complaint, diagnosis with ICD-10 code, procedure, implant details, admission, operation and discharge date and time, medico-legal (yes or no), condition at discharge, medicines and follow-up.
  8. Final bill with itemised break-up and payment receipt (mode, date, amount in figures and words).
  9. Prescriptions and pharmacy bills for drops and tablets, with the patient's name, dated within the policy's pre- and post-hospitalisation period (commonly 30 and 60 days).
  10. Claim form, hospital part (often Part B) filled and signed by the treating doctor with clinic stamp; the patient fills the insured's part.
  11. From the patient: policy copy or e-card, photo ID, KYC and cancelled cheque or bank details (for reimbursement).

Standard bill codes for eye surgery

Most insurers and TPAs accept bills in the standard provider format of IRDAI's health insurance standardisation guidelines (Schedule D). Each line carries a primary code for its head and a code for the item. The generator above fills these in; this is the subset an eye clinic uses most.

  • Itemise the heads the insurer expects: consultation, investigations, surgeon's fee, IOL, OT, room or day-care bed, anaesthesia, medicines. Package bills should still show what the package includes.
  • Bill the IOL at its actual price and attach its invoice; insurers compare.
  • Do not add separate charges for non-payable items (gloves, masks, drapes, admission kits); IRDAI lists these as non-payable unless part of the procedure charge.
  • Room charges for a day-care stay should be reasonable; very high room rent can trigger proportionate deductions under some policies.
  • Write the amount in words, sign and stamp. Avoid handwritten corrections; reissue a corrected bill instead.
  • Do not accept cash of ₹2 lakh or more for one transaction (Income Tax Act, section 269ST); prefer UPI, card or cheque, and record the mode.

Case-by-case checklist

CaseExtra papers and points
Cataract, monofocal IOLBiometry, IOL sticker and invoice; diagnosis "age-related cataract, left eye" with ICD-10 H25 (for example H25.1 nuclear); vision before surgery. Check the policy's cataract sub-limit (often a fixed amount or percentage per eye) and waiting period (commonly 1 to 2 years) and tell the patient their share before surgery.
Cataract with toric, multifocal or EDOF IOLMany policies pay only up to a standard lens or the cataract sub-limit. Show the IOL as one line at its real price, explain the patient's extra cost in writing before surgery, and keep the signed acknowledgement.
Both eyesSeparate files, bills and discharge summaries for each eye and each date. Each eye may have its own sub-limit.
Intravitreal injections (anti-VEGF, steroid implants)A recognised "modern treatment" under IRDAI product rules (sub-limits may apply). Each injection is a separate day-care claim: OCT report showing the indication, drug invoice with batch number, injection note, and the treatment plan (number of doses). See the anti-VEGF planner.
Pterygium with conjunctival autograftShow it is not cosmetic: size, encroachment towards the visual axis, induced astigmatism, recurrence or irritation, with a clinical photo.
Glaucoma surgery or vitreoretinal surgeryDetailed indication (IOP history and fields, or OCT and B-scan), operative notes, implant or tamponade invoices (shunts, silicone oil).
Eye injuryTime and mode of injury; whether it is a medico-legal case; police intimation or FIR for assault or road accidents; photos of the injury.
LASIK, PRK, SMILEExcluded under IRDAI standard exclusions when the refractive error is less than 7.5 D; for higher errors, give refraction and topography and check the policy.
YAG capsulotomy, laser, minor procedures in the OPDUsually payable only under OPD cover or if listed as day care by the policy; check before promising.
Squint and oculoplastic surgeryCosmetic procedures are excluded; document functional reasons (double vision, visual axis covered by the lid, field loss with photos and fields).

IRDAI's 2024 master circular on health insurance requires cashless decisions within 1 hour and discharge authorisation within 3 hours, asks insurers and TPAs to collect documents from hospitals rather than burden patients, and requires a claims review committee to approve any rejection.

Never change admission or surgery dates, show day care as a longer admission, bill items not used, inflate the IOL price, or split one procedure into several bills. Insurers audit eye claims closely, and false documents can lead to criminal charges, removal from networks and loss of reputation.

Keeping records

  • Keep a copy of every claim file (scanned in your EMR) for at least as long as your record-retention policy; see medical records.
  • Keep a register of cashless claims: patient, insurer or TPA, claim number, approved amount, settled amount, deductions and date paid. Follow up unpaid claims monthly.

General guidance for clinicians in India; not legal or insurance advice. Policies, sub-limits and insurer requirements vary; always check the patient's policy wording and the insurer's or TPA's current checklist.
