IOL Power Calculator: SRK/T, Holladay 1, Hoffer Q and Haigis Side by Side, with Toric IOL Suggestion

For: PG residents · Practising ophthalmologists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Five published formulas side by side: SRK/T, Holladay 1, Hoffer Q, Haigis and SRK II (teaching).
  • Shows the suggested IOL power for your target and the expected refraction for nearby powers.
  • Suggests a toric IOL when corneal astigmatism, after the incision, is at least 1.00 D with-the-rule or oblique, or 0.75 D against-the-rule.
  • Flags unusual biometry, differences between eyes, long eyes and previous refractive surgery; gives the sulcus power.
  • For learning and cross-checking: use Barrett, Kane or EVO online and your biometer for the final decision.

This calculator works out IOL power with five published formulas side by side: SRK/T, Holladay 1, Hoffer Q, Haigis and SRK II. It suggests the power closest to your target, shows the expected refraction for nearby powers, flags unusual biometry, gives the sulcus power, and tells you when a toric IOL should be preferred.

Use it to learn and to cross-check, not as your only calculation. Newer formulas such as Barrett Universal II, Kane, EVO and Hill-RBF are more accurate in most eyes, but their equations are not published, so they cannot be reproduced here. Use them on their official websites (links below) and in your biometer.

Enter biometry for the eye to be operated. Results update as you type. Nothing is stored or sent.

Toric planning: incision and surgically induced astigmatism

For a temporal incision in the right eye use 180°, left eye 0° (or your own meridian). Use your own measured SIA if you know it.

More options: other eye, age, long eyes, Haigis constants

For learning and cross-checking only. Base surgical decisions on your biometer's software and validated online calculators, with lens constants optimised for your IOL.

Official online calculators for newer formulas

  • Barrett Universal II, free on the APACRS website (Calculators section), and the Barrett Toric calculator
  • Kane formula
  • EVO formula
  • ASCRS post-refractive IOL calculator
  • Also see our page of IOL and toric IOL calculators.

How to read the results

  • Exact power is the unrounded power for your target.
  • Suggested IOL is the available power (in 0.5 D steps) whose expected refraction is closest to the target, leaning slightly myopic when two are equally close.
  • Nearby powers show the expected spectacle refraction for 1.0 D either side, so you can choose for monovision or a second lens model.
  • A green tick marks the formulas that suit this axial length. If they disagree by more than 0.5 D, re-check the biometry before choosing.
  • The copy summary button gives a one-line note for your EMR or case sheet.

Which formula for which eye

Axial lengthFormulas that perform well (among these five)Notes
Below 22 mm (short)Hoffer Q, HaigisSmall errors in ELP matter most; Barrett and Kane are preferred where available
22 to 24.5 mm (average)SRK/T, Holladay 1, Hoffer Q, HaigisAll similar; optimise your lens constant
24.5 to 26 mmSRK/T, Holladay 1, Haigis
Above 26 mm (long)SRK/T, HaigisHolladay 1 and Hoffer Q tend to leave hyperopia unless the Wang-Koch adjustment is applied; consider a slightly myopic target
After LASIK, PRK or RKNone of theseUse post-refractive methods (Barrett True-K, Haigis-L, Shammas) on the ASCRS calculator

See also SRK II, SRK/T, Hoffer Q and Holladay 1 compared and the biometry checklist.

Toric IOL: when the calculator suggests one

  • A toric IOL is suggested when corneal astigmatism, after allowing for your incision, is 1.00 D or more with-the-rule or oblique, or 0.75 D or more against-the-rule. The lower against-the-rule threshold allows for posterior corneal astigmatism, which adds against-the-rule cylinder that front-surface keratometry misses.
  • Between 0.25 D below the threshold and the threshold, it suggests a borderline decision: a low-power toric or limbal relaxing incisions.
  • The IOL-plane cylinder is estimated as about 1.46 times the corneal cylinder. Always confirm the toric model and axis with the Barrett Toric or manufacturer's calculator, which include posterior corneal astigmatism and your actual IOL.
  • Check that keratometry from two devices agrees in magnitude and axis before ordering a toric lens (use the keratometry tools).

Lens constants

  • Enter the A-constant for SRK/T from the lens box, the manufacturer, or better, an optimised constant from IOLCon or your own outcomes. See the A-constant reference.
  • Holladay surgeon factor, Hoffer pACD and SRK/T ACD constant are converted from the A-constant using the standard Retzlaff and Holladay relationships.
  • For Haigis, enter optimised a0, a1 and a2 for your lens if you have them. Otherwise a0 is estimated from the A-constant (with a1 = 0.4 and a2 = 0.1) so that Haigis matches SRK/T in an average eye; this is an approximation.
  • Optical biometry constants differ from ultrasound (applanation) constants by about 0.3 to 0.4 in the A-constant.
