OKAP, FRCOphth and ICO Exams: Formats and How to Prepare with RPC Rounds

Last updated: October 2026

Short answer: the OKAP (American Academy of Ophthalmology), FRCOphth (Royal College of Ophthalmologists, UK) and ICO examinations test the same core ophthalmology, but in different formats. This page summarises each exam from its official information and shows which RPC Rounds articles help with which part. RPC Rounds is written mainly for Indian MS/DNB residents. It is not an official or complete review course for any of these exams, so use it alongside the recommended textbooks and the exam's own syllabus.

Exam formats were checked against the official AAO, RCOphth and ICO websites in October 2026. Formats, fees and dates change, so always confirm on the official site before you register.

The three exams at a glance

ExamRun byFormatWho takes it
OKAPAmerican Academy of Ophthalmology260 multiple-choice questions spread equally across the 13 BCSC sections; 4 hours 30 minutes of testing plus an optional 10-minute break; onlineResidents in every year of training in the US and Canada. OKAP International uses the same 260-question format with remote proctoring and is open to residents and practising ophthalmologists worldwide.
FRCOphthRoyal College of Ophthalmologists (UK)Part 1 (written), Refraction Certificate (practical), Part 2 Written, and Part 2 Oral (viva and OSCE)UK ophthalmic specialist trainees, and other eligible candidates
ICO examinationsInternational Council of OphthalmologyStandard Examinations (Visual Sciences; Optics, Refraction and Instruments; Clinical Ophthalmology), then the Advanced ExaminationOphthalmologists and trainees worldwide; taken in English

OKAP: the 13 BCSC sections and where to start on RPC Rounds

OKAP questions are spread equally across the 13 sections of the AAO's Basic and Clinical Science Course (BCSC). The coverage column is our honest estimate of how much RPC Rounds offers for each section.

FRCOphth: each part and how RPC Rounds can help

PartOfficial formatUseful RPC Rounds articles
Part 1 FRCOphthTwo multiple-choice papers of 90 questions each, two hours per paper, on the same day. Tests basic and clinical sciences and understanding of patient investigations; no clinical component.Basic sciences, pharmacology, retinal layers, electro-oculography. Coverage of anatomy, pathology and statistics is limited, so rely on textbooks for these.
Refraction CertificateA practical exam. The October 2025 New Delhi sitting had 10 OSCE stations: simulated retinoscopy (4), non-cycloplegic retinoscopy (2), subjective refraction (sphere and cylinder), lens neutralisation, and binocular balancing.Retinoscopy tips, JCC, duochrome, optics lectures. Reading is no substitute for practising on real patients and a model eye.
Part 2 WrittenTwo multiple-choice papers of 90 questions each, delivered online, usually twice a year. Tests applied clinical knowledge across the curriculum.Use the learning dashboard by subspecialty, plus landmark trials and gradings and staging systems.
Part 2 OralA structured viva (five 10-minute stations: investigations and data interpretation; two patient management stations; attitudes, ethics and responsibilities; audit, research, evidence-based practice and health promotion) and an OSCE (five 20-minute clinical stations: anterior segment; glaucoma and lid; posterior segment; strabismus and orbit; neuro-ophthalmology; plus a communication skills station).History and case presentation, reading an OCT, reading a visual field, emergencies on call, spot the diagnosis. The ethics and UK practice stations are not covered here.

One change to note: the ICO states that from 1 January 2028 the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Glasgow will no longer accept ICO examination qualifications as exemptions for its FRCS Ophthalmology examinations.

A practical study approach

  • Use the official syllabus as your checklist. Tick off each topic as you cover it, from any source.
  • Build on Indian MS/DNB preparation. The clinical content overlaps heavily; the differences are mainly in format and in UK or US practice guidelines.
  • Practise the format. Single-best-answer questions reward reading every option; OSCEs reward a structured, spoken examination routine.
  • Test yourself regularly. Try the clinical pearls quizzes, the gradings flip cards and the RPC question bank.

Free mock tests

Check your level with a timed 50-question mock: the OKAP-style mock (about four questions from each BCSC section) and the FRCOphth Part 1 and ICO basic sciences mock, the FRCOphth Part 2 Written mock, the ICO optics mock and the ABO WQE-style mock. Each gives a topic-wise score and explains every answer. For a much larger bank, see OKAT Decoded (10,200 original MCQs written for the Indian AIOS-OKAT, covering the same core curriculum).

Frequently asked questions

Can Indian residents take the OKAP?

The standard OKAP is for residents in US and Canadian programmes. The AAO's OKAP International uses the same 260-question format, is open to residents and practising ophthalmologists worldwide, and is taken with remote proctoring. Check the AAO website for eligibility and fees for your country.

How many questions are in Part 1 FRCOphth?

Two multiple-choice papers of 90 questions each, two hours per paper, taken on the same day.

What does the FRCOphth Refraction Certificate involve?

It is a practical OSCE. The October 2025 New Delhi sitting had 10 stations covering simulated and non-cycloplegic retinoscopy, subjective refraction, lens neutralisation and binocular balancing.

How do you get FICO?

By passing the ICO Standard Examinations, then the Advanced Examination together with a local face-to-face ophthalmology examination.

Is RPC Rounds enough to pass these exams?

No. It is a free teaching blog that covers much of the clinical content, but it does not replace the official syllabus, standard textbooks or practice in each exam's format.
