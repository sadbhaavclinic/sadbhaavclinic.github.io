Diagnostic Criteria in Ophthalmology: Marfan, VKH, NF1, NMOSD and More

Last updated: October 2026

Diagnostic criteria and screening rules that ophthalmologists use, updated to the current versions. Older versions are noted where exam questions may still use them.

  • Vogt–Koyanagi–Harada disease

Family history of Marfan5. Ectopia lentis

Vogt–Koyanagi–Harada disease

Category (Revised Diagnostic Criteria, 2001)Required
Complete VKHCriteria 1–5
Incomplete VKHCriteria 1–3 plus either 4 or 5
Probable VKH (eye only)Criteria 1–3
Criterion 1No history of penetrating trauma or eye surgery
Criterion 2No other eye disease
Criterion 3Bilateral uveitis: early (diffuse choroiditis, serous detachments) or late (sunset glow, Dalen–Fuchs-like nodules)
Criterion 4Neurological or auditory signs (meningism, tinnitus, CSF pleocytosis)
Criterion 5Skin signs after the eye or CNS disease (alopecia, poliosis, vitiligo)

The SUN Working Group classification criteria (2021) split VKH into early-stage and late-stage disease and are used in research.

Neurofibromatosis type 1 (revised 2021)

Two or more of the following (or one, if a parent has NF1):

FeatureDetail
Café-au-lait macules6 or more: over 5 mm before puberty, over 15 mm after
FrecklingAxillary or inguinal (bilateral if only pigmentary signs)
Neurofibromas2 or more of any type, or 1 plexiform
Optic pathway gliomaUsually found on MRI or by visual loss
Iris or choroidal signs2 or more Lisch nodules on slit lamp, or 2 or more choroidal abnormalities on OCT or near-infrared imaging
Distinctive bone lesionSphenoid dysplasia, anterolateral tibial bowing or long-bone pseudarthrosis
Genetic testHeterozygous pathogenic NF1 variant

Thyroid eye disease (Bartley and Gorman, 1995)

Eyelid retraction with objective thyroid dysfunction or one other sign (exophthalmos, optic neuropathy or extraocular muscle involvement); or, without lid retraction, thyroid dysfunction plus one of those signs. Other causes must be excluded.

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (IPND 2015)

GroupCriteria
AQP4-IgG positiveAt least 1 core clinical characteristic, and alternative diagnoses excluded
AQP4-IgG negative or unknownAt least 2 core characteristics from separate attacks, including optic neuritis, longitudinally extensive myelitis or area postrema syndrome, with supporting MRI findings
Core clinical characteristicsOptic neuritis; acute myelitis; area postrema syndrome (hiccups, vomiting); brainstem syndrome; narcolepsy or diencephalic syndrome; cerebral syndrome with typical lesions

MOG antibody disease (MOGAD) has its own criteria (2023). The old Wingerchuk 2006 criteria (optic neuritis + myelitis + 2 of 3 supporting features) are now historical.

Diabetes: diagnosis, targets and eye screening (ADA)

ItemCurrent standard
Diagnosis (any one)Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L); 2-hour OGTT glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L); HbA1c ≥ 6.5%; random glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms
Targets for most adultsHbA1c under 7%; blood pressure under 130/80 mmHg; LDL cholesterol under 70 mg/dL (under 55 mg/dL with cardiovascular disease)
First eye exam: type 1Within 5 years of diagnosis
First eye exam: type 2At diagnosis
Follow-upYearly; every 1–2 years if no retinopathy and good control; more often if retinopathy progresses
PregnancyBefore conception or in the first trimester, then each trimester and for 1 year after delivery

Fundus photography for DR: 7 standard fields

The Airlie House (DRS) protocol used seven 30° stereo fields; the ETDRS modified it to seven 35° fields, still the research standard. Wide-field and ultra-wide-field imaging now often replace it.

FieldCentred on
1Optic disc
2Macula
3Temporal to the macula
4Superotemporal, tangent to the disc
5Inferotemporal
6Superonasal
7Inferonasal

Lamellar macular hole (consensus definition, 2020)

EntityOCT criteria
Lamellar macular holeMandatory: irregular foveal contour; foveal cavity with undermined edges; at least one sign of foveal tissue loss (pseudo-operculum, central foveal thinning, ellipsoid zone defect). Optional: epiretinal proliferation, foveal bump
Epiretinal membrane foveoschisisContractile ERM; schisis at the level of Henle fibre layer; may have microcystoid spaces in the INL
Macular pseudoholeFoveal-sparing ERM with verticalised foveal edges; near-normal central thickness

Fraser (cryptophthalmos) syndrome (Thomas criteria)

At least 2 major and 1 minor criterion, or 1 major and 4 minor. Major: cryptophthalmos, syndactyly, abnormal genitalia, affected sibling. Minor: malformations of the nose, ears or larynx, cleft lip or palate, skeletal defects, umbilical hernia, renal agenesis, intellectual disability. A revised scheme (van Haelst, 2007) adds urinary tract and laryngeal anomalies as major criteria.
