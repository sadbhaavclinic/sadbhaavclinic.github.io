Eye Treatment for International Patients

Typical stay, costs and how to start

Eye treatment at Sadbhaav is led by Dr Dhaval Patel, MD (Ophthalmology, AIIMS New Delhi), a cataract and refractive surgeon. International patients start with a free review of their reports, so you know the plan, the time needed and the estimated cost before you travel.

Treatments and typical time in Ahmedabad

TreatmentTypical stayNotes
Cataract surgery (phacoemulsification with a foldable lens)About 1 week for one eye; about 10–14 days if both eyes are doneDay-care surgery. Reviewed the next day and again before you fly. The second eye is usually done a few days to a week after the first.
LASIK and laser vision correctionAbout 5–7 daysDetailed pre-operative tests, including corneal topography, are done first and decide whether laser is safe for you.

Squint (strabismus) surgeryAbout 1 weekMeasurements are repeated on arrival to confirm the surgical plan.

Reports that help us plan

  • Cataract: vision and glasses prescription; biometry if done; details of other eye conditions such as glaucoma or diabetic eye disease.
  • LASIK, ICL or keratoconus: corneal topography or tomography (Pentacam or similar), glasses prescription and how long it has been stable, and whether you wear contact lenses.
  • Glaucoma: eye pressure readings, visual field reports, OCT of the optic nerve and the drops you use.
  • Retina: OCT scans, fundus photographs or angiography reports.

Flying after eye treatment

  • After cataract surgery, LASIK and most other eye operations, flying home is usually fine once your doctor has reviewed you, typically after about a week.
  • Do not fly with a gas bubble in the eye (used in some retinal operations): the bubble expands at altitude and can cause a dangerous rise in eye pressure. Your doctor will tell you when it is safe.
  • Carry your eye drops in your hand luggage with the written prescription, and use them on the schedule given, even during travel.

Before you travel

  • Bring all previous eye reports, your current glasses and a list of medicines.
  • Keep diabetes and blood pressure well controlled; uncontrolled readings can delay surgery.
  • Plan a few extra days in case your review shows that more time is needed.
