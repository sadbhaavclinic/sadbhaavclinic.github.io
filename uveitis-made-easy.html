Uveitis Made Easy: SUN Terms, Patterns and Work-up

Last updated: October 2026

Uveitis feels endless because there are dozens of causes. Senior uveitis specialists cut it down with a few questions: where is the inflammation, what does it look like, who is the patient, and could it be infection or something pretending to be uveitis? The answers point to a short list and a short set of tests.

Five rules

1. Name it by where it is (SUN)Anterior = anterior chamber. Intermediate = vitreous and pars plana. Posterior = retina or choroid. Panuveitis = all three. Cells in the vitreous spilling over from the front do not make it intermediate.
2. Granulomatous or notMutton-fat KPs, Koeppe (pupil margin) or Busacca (iris surface) nodules mean sarcoid, TB, syphilis, VKH, sympathetic ophthalmia, herpes. Fine KPs with fibrin suggest HLA-B27.
3. Age, eye and course narrow it furtherChild with a white eye: JIA. Young adult, one eye at a time, sudden, recurrent: HLA-B27. Over 50 with vitreous cells: think lymphoma.
4. Infection firstBefore steroids, ask: could this be syphilis, TB, toxoplasma, herpes, CMV or endophthalmitis? Every patient needs syphilis serology; in India, also screen for TB.
5. Tailor the testsOrder tests for the pattern you see. A shotgun panel (ANA, RF, ACE for everyone) gives false positives and no answers.

Masquerade syndromes

  • Older adults: primary vitreoretinal lymphoma, metastases, uveal melanoma, retained intraocular foreign body, chronic endophthalmitis (Cutibacterium after cataract surgery).
  • Children: retinoblastoma (pseudohypopyon), leukaemia, juvenile xanthogranuloma (spontaneous hyphaema).
  • Any age: rhegmatogenous RD with AC cells (Schwartz–Matsuo), pigment dispersion, ocular ischaemic syndrome.

Treatment in steps

StepOptionsPoints to remember
LocalTopical prednisolone acetate 1% and cycloplegics; periocular triamcinolone; intravitreal dexamethasone implant or fluocinolone implantWatch IOP (steroid response) and cataract
Systemic steroidOral prednisolone about 1 mg/kg/day, tapering; IV methylprednisolone for VKH or severe diseaseAim for ≤7.5 mg/day within 3 months, or add a steroid-sparing drug
Steroid-sparingMethotrexate, mycophenolate, azathioprine, ciclosporin, tacrolimusTakes 2–3 months to work
BiologicsAdalimumab (VISUAL I and II, SYCAMORE for JIA), infliximab (Behçet), tocilizumabScreen for TB, hepatitis B and C, and HIV first; avoid anti-TNF in MS
  • Cystoid macular oedema is the commonest cause of permanent vision loss in uveitis.
  • JIA screening: highest risk (ANA-positive, oligoarthritis, onset before 7, within 4 years of onset) needs a slit-lamp check about every 3 months, because the eye is white and quiet.
  • Fuchs uveitis does not need long-term steroids, and does well with cataract surgery.

Where residents slip

  • Starting steroids for a retinitis without thinking of ARN, CMV, syphilis or toxoplasma.
  • Calling vitreous spill-over from severe anterior uveitis “intermediate uveitis”.
  • Ordering the same big panel for everyone instead of testing for the pattern.
  • Treating Fuchs uveitis with long courses of steroids.
  • Missing lymphoma in an older patient whose “uveitis” keeps coming back as steroids are tapered.
