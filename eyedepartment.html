Technology Available

What Do We Have

Digital Slit Lamp Camera System

Appasamy INDIA

Your eye examination microscope with a built-in camera. We can photograph or video your cornea, lens, iris and retina, show you on screen exactly what we see, and compare pictures at your next visit. It helps with cataract, corneal ulcers, uveitis and diabetic eye disease, and the photos can be shared for a second opinion or online consultation.

OCTavius OCT

Huvitz, South Korea

A painless scan, like a light-based ultrasound, that shows the layers of your retina and optic nerve in fine detail. It helps find and follow macular disease, diabetic swelling and glaucoma. What to expect in an OCT scan.

Optical Biometer

Huvitz, South Korea

Measures your eye with light, without touching it, to calculate the power of the lens implant before cataract surgery. Accurate numbers mean less chance of needing glasses afterwards. How your lens power is calculated.

OCT Angiography

Huvitz, South Korea

Shows the tiny blood vessels of the retina and the layer beneath it, without any dye injection, so there is no risk of a dye reaction. It helps find and follow diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration (including new abnormal vessels), retinal vein blockages and glaucoma, often picking up small changes that ordinary photographs miss. Because it is safe to repeat at every visit, it also shows how well treatment such as eye injections is working. More on OCT angiography.

Placido Topography

Huvitz, South Korea

Maps the shape and power of the front surface of your eye (cornea) as colour pictures. It detects keratoconus and other irregular corneas early, often before a routine examination shows them; checks whether LASIK is safe for you; gives corneal measurements for cataract lens calculation; helps fit special contact lenses; and tracks progression and the effect of C3R treatment for keratoconus. More on corneal topography.

Non Contact Tonometer with Pachy

Huvitz, South Korea

Checks eye pressure with a gentle puff of air: no drops and nothing touches your eye, so it suits all ages. The machine lines itself up automatically and averages several readings for accuracy, and it also measures corneal thickness, which helps us read the pressure correctly. Key for early detection and follow-up of glaucoma. More on the eye pressure test.

Perimetry Machine

APPASAMY India

Tests your whole field of vision, including side vision, while you press a button each time you see a small light. Glaucoma steals side vision without you noticing, so this test finds damage early, before serious loss, and repeat tests show whether treatment is working. It is also used for some nerve, brain and retinal diseases. More on the visual field test.

Fundus Photo

Huvitz, South Korea

Takes colour photographs of the back of your eye: retina, optic nerve and blood vessels. They help detect diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, macular degeneration and blood-pressure changes early, let us compare your eye from visit to visit, and let you see your own eye, which makes the treatment plan easier to understand. More on fundus photography.

YAG Laser Machine

Appasamy, India

A painless outpatient laser, with no cut and no operation theatre. It clears the cloudy membrane ("secondary cataract") that can form months or years after cataract surgery, restoring clear vision, and makes a tiny opening in the iris to prevent or treat angle-closure glaucoma by lowering eye pressure. More on YAG laser.

5 Step Slit Lamp

Appasamy, India

The standard eye examination microscope, with five magnification steps for a close, detailed look at every part of your eye.

Auto Ref-Keratometer HRK-8000A

Huvitz, Korea

Gives a quick, computerised first estimate of your glasses number and measures the curve of your cornea. The doctor or optometrist then fine-tunes the final prescription.

Compact Touch A scan

Quantel Medical, France

An ultrasound measurement of the length of your eye, used for lens power before cataract surgery when a dense cataract blocks the light-based biometer.

Lumera 300 Microscope

Zeiss, Germany

The Zeiss operating microscope in our eye theatre. Its bright, clear view lets the surgeon work precisely through very small openings during cataract and other eye surgery.

Catarhex Phaco System

Oertli, Switzerland

The Swiss machine we use for stitch-free phaco cataract surgery. It works through small openings of about 2 mm, which helps faster healing.

Algerbrush Diamond Burr

Alger Company, USA

A tiny, gentle rotating tool used in the clinic, with numbing drops, to remove foreign bodies and the rust rings left by metal particles in the eye, and to smooth the corneal surface in recurrent corneal erosion and some corneal dystrophies. It is more controlled than a needle or blade, so it is quicker, more comfortable and helps the cornea heal faster.

CCQ-800 Auto Lensmeter

Yeasn, PRC

Reads the exact power of your existing glasses in seconds (including cylinder, axis and prism), checks that new glasses match your prescription before you take them home, and can test how much UV light the lenses block.

Cyberchart Digital Vision Chart

AKAS, India

A digital screen instead of a printed wall chart. It shows letters, pictures for children, colour-vision and contrast tests, so testing is quicker and more consistent.

Indirect Ophthalmoscope

Appasamy, India

A head-mounted light used with a hand-held lens to examine the whole retina, right to its edges, after the pupils are dilated. Essential for retinal tears and diabetic eye checks.

Axis Pachymetry

Quantel Medical, France

Measures the thickness of your cornea very precisely with a small ultrasound probe after numbing drops. Needed before LASIK and other laser vision surgery, and to correct the eye pressure reading in glaucoma, because a thick or thin cornea can make the pressure read falsely high or low.

Goldmann 3-Mirror Lens

Volk, USA

A special examination lens placed gently on the numbed eye. Its three angled mirrors let the doctor see the centre of the retina, its far edges and the eye’s drainage angle, to find retinal tears, weak areas (lattice), detachment, diabetic changes and glaucoma-related angle changes. It is also used during laser treatments such as retinal laser for diabetes and sealing retinal tears.

Posner Indentation Gonioscope

Ocular, USA

A small lens used with numbing drops to look at the eye’s drainage angle. It tells us whether the angle is open or narrow, and, by pressing gently, whether a narrow angle can still open, which decides the type of glaucoma treatment, including laser iridotomy or surgery. It also shows abnormal vessels, foreign bodies or growths in the angle, and checks how well glaucoma surgery is working.

Electronic Medical Record System

Raj Patel & Dhaval Patel

Our own computerised records system. You receive a printed report of your examination, treatment and instructions, and your earlier findings are always available at follow-up.

Your first visit to the eye department

A few simple things make your first eye check smoother. For the step-by-step journey, see your visit to the eye department.

How long it takes

Allow about 1½ to 2 hours for a first full eye check. If your pupils need dilating drops, they take 20–30 minutes to work before the doctor can examine the back of your eye.

Dilating drops

The drops blur near vision and make light uncomfortable for about 4–6 hours. Do not drive yourself home: come with someone, and bring sunglasses. More about dilating drops.

What to bring

  • Your current glasses (and contact lens box or details)
  • Old eye reports, prescriptions and scans
  • A list of all your medicines, especially blood thinners, diabetes and prostate medicines
  • Health insurance card, if surgery may be discussed

Good to know

  • Contact lens wearers coming for a glasses, LASIK or cataract check: ask us how many days to leave lenses out first.
  • Children: bring a favourite toy or snack; drops may be needed for an accurate glasses check.
  • Fees for the consultation and any test are told to you before anything is done. Tests such as OCT are done only when needed.
