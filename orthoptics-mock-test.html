Orthoptics Mock Test 1 (Mixed): 30 MCQs with Answers

For: Allied eye-care staff

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • 30 original questions covering the orthoptics study course.
  • Answer all, then press "Check my score": correct answers turn green, wrong ones red.
  • Open "Show answer" for the explanation.
  • Revise with the study course.

Study courses for eye-care careers: Ophthalmic Nursing · Orthoptics · Ophthalmic Dispensing · Ocularistry · Eye Banking · Ophthalmic Photography and Imaging · Low Vision and Vision Rehabilitation · Eye Care Support Roles · Ophthalmic technician · All careers

Set a timer for 30 minutes. The option order is mixed, so read every choice.

1. Hering's law states that:

Show answer

Answer: Yoke muscles receive equal innervation. Explains secondary deviation larger than primary in paralytic squint.

2. Sherrington's law refers to:

Show answer

Answer: Reciprocal inhibition of the antagonist in the same eye. When the agonist contracts the antagonist relaxes.

3. The primary action of the superior oblique is:

Show answer

Answer: Intorsion. Also depression (in adduction) and abduction.

4. The primary action of the inferior oblique is:

Show answer

Answer: Extorsion. Also elevation in adduction and abduction.

5. A fourth nerve palsy patient often tilts the head:

Show answer

Answer: Away from the affected side. To reduce vertical diplopia; Bielschowsky head tilt test positive to the same side.

6. Sixth nerve palsy causes:

Show answer

Answer: Limited abduction and esotropia worse for distance. Horizontal diplopia worse in the affected direction.

7. Complete third nerve palsy causes the eye to be:

Show answer

Answer: Down and out with ptosis. Pupil involvement suggests compressive cause: urgent.

8. Accommodative esotropia is usually associated with:

Show answer

Answer: Hypermetropia. Full cycloplegic correction is the first treatment.

9. Intermittent exotropia typically appears:

Show answer

Answer: When the child is tired, ill or looking at distance. Parents notice one eye drifting outwards in bright light or distance.

10. The prism cover test measures:

Show answer

Answer: Total deviation (tropia + phoria). Alternate cover with prisms.

11. The 4 prism dioptre base-out test detects:

Show answer

Answer: Small-angle squint with central suppression (microtropia). No refixation movement suggests a foveal suppression scotoma.

12. Normal near point of convergence is about:

Show answer

Answer: Within 6–10 cm. A remote NPC suggests convergence insufficiency.

13. Convergence insufficiency is best treated with:

Show answer

Answer: Convergence exercises (office-based therapy works best in children). CITT evidence.

14. Brown syndrome is:

Show answer

Answer: Limited elevation in adduction from superior oblique tendon restriction. A restrictive disorder.

15. Duane syndrome type 1 shows:

Show answer

Answer: Limited abduction with globe retraction on adduction. Congenital miswiring of the sixth nerve territory.

16. Thyroid eye disease most commonly restricts the:

Show answer

Answer: Inferior rectus. Causing limited elevation; medial rectus next.

17. Hess chart smaller field indicates:

Show answer

Answer: The affected (underacting) eye. The eye with the smaller field has the paresis.

18. The Worth four-dot test with three green and two red lights means:

Show answer

Answer: Diplopia. Five lights indicate diplopia.

19. Stereoacuity is measured in:

Show answer

Answer: Seconds of arc. Normal adult stereo about 40–60 seconds of arc.

20. The most effective first treatment of refractive amblyopia is:

Show answer

Answer: Full-time optimal glasses. PEDIG showed glasses alone improve many children.

21. Atropine penalisation is placed in the:

Show answer

Answer: Better (fellow) eye. Blurs the good eye at near.

22. Dissociated vertical deviation (DVD) shows:

Show answer

Answer: Upward drift of either eye when covered, without corresponding downward movement of the other. Often with infantile esotropia.

23. An A pattern means the horizontal deviation is:

Show answer

Answer: More eso in upgaze (or more exo in downgaze). A: eyes converge more on looking up.

24. Internuclear ophthalmoplegia causes:

Show answer

Answer: Limited adduction of one eye with nystagmus of the abducting eye. Lesion of the medial longitudinal fasciculus (e.g. MS, stroke).

25. Myasthenia gravis diplopia is typically:

Show answer

Answer: Variable and worse with fatigue, often with ptosis. Ice test and antibody testing help.

26. The diplopia test before squint surgery in adults checks:

Show answer

Answer: Risk of intractable double vision after correction. Using prisms to simulate the correction.

27. Botulinum toxin for squint is injected into:

Show answer

Answer: An extraocular muscle. Temporarily weakens the muscle.

28. A child with a constant squint after age 4 months should:

Show answer

Answer: Have a full eye examination including cycloplegic refraction and fundus. Exclude cataract, retinoblastoma and refractive causes.

29. Pseudo-esotropia is distinguished by:

Show answer

Answer: Cover test showing no movement and symmetrical light reflexes. Broad nasal bridge and epicanthus.

30. The fusional vergence that compensates an exophoria is:

Show answer

Answer: Convergent (positive) fusional reserve. Base-out prism measures it.
