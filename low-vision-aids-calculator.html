Low Vision Aids Calculator: Kestenbaum Rule and Magnification

Last updated: October 2026

Clinic Desk ReferenceLow Vision

A starting point for magnifiers and telescopes from the patient's acuity, with the formulas behind it. Always confirm with trial aids and the patient's real tasks.

The formulas

  • Kestenbaum's rule: the reciprocal of the distance Snellen fraction gives the add (in dioptres) needed to read 1M print. Example: 6/60 → 60 ÷ 6 = +10 D.
  • Magnification needed for near: current print size ÷ goal print size (at the same distance). Example: reads 4M, wants 1M → 4×. Add a reserve so the patient reads comfortably, not at threshold (aim to read print 2–3 times smaller than the goal).
  • Magnifier power: conventional magnification = F ÷ 4 (so 4× ≈ +16 D). Working distance with a high add = 100 ÷ add in cm (+10 D → 10 cm).
  • Telescopes for distance: magnification = current acuity ÷ target acuity, e.g. 6/60 → 6/12 needs 5×.

Choosing an aid

TaskOptions
ReadingHigh-add reading glasses (with base-in prism for binocular use up to about +10 D), hand or stand magnifiers with good lighting, electronic magnifiers (CCTV), phone or tablet zoom and text-to-speech
Distance (TV, signs, board at school)Hand-held monocular telescopes (2.5×–8×), spectacle-mounted telescopes, sitting closer, phone camera zoom
Glare and contrastTinted filters (amber, yellow), caps and visors, high-contrast markings at home
Field lossScanning training, prisms (limited benefit), orientation and mobility training
Daily livingLarge-print phones and watches, talking devices, accessibility settings, bump-ons for appliances

Do not forget: refract carefully first (low vision patients often gain from an updated prescription), good task lighting, a disability certificate if eligible (see the calculator), and referral for rehabilitation, education support and counselling.

Related: acuity conversion, levels of visual impairment, what 6/60 means (for patients).

Key sources

  • Dickinson C. Low Vision: Principles and Practice. Butterworth-Heinemann; 1998
  • Kestenbaum A, Sturman RM. Reading glasses for patients with very poor vision. Arch Ophthalmol 1956;56:451–70
  • American Academy of Ophthalmology. Vision Rehabilitation Preferred Practice Pattern (2022)
