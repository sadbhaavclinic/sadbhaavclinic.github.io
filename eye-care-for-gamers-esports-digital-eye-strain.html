Eye Care for Gamers and Esports Players: Digital Eye Strain, Dry Eye, Blue Light Myths, VR Headsets and Screen Setup

For: Patients and families

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Long gaming sessions cut the blink rate and keep the eyes focused close up, causing dry, tired, burning eyes, blur and headache (digital eye strain).
  • Fix the basics: correct glasses, screen at arm's length and slightly below eye level, brightness matched to the room, and regular breaks.
  • Try the 20-20-20 habit: every 20 minutes look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds, and blink fully.
  • Blue-light-filtering glasses have not been shown to reduce eye strain; night mode may help sleep.
  • Children who game a lot should still get about 2 hours outdoors daily to lower the risk of myopia.

Gaming and esports demand long hours of intense focus on a bright screen a short distance away. The eyes are not damaged by this, but they do get tired, dry and uncomfortable, and performance suffers. A few simple changes make a big difference.

What is digital eye strain?

  • Symptoms: dry, gritty, burning or watery eyes; blurred vision, especially when looking up from the screen; headache around the brows; neck and shoulder ache; eye twitching.
  • Why it happens: the blink rate drops sharply during concentrated screen use, and many blinks are incomplete, so the tear film evaporates. The focusing muscles and the muscles that turn the eyes in work hard for hours at a fixed distance. Glare and a screen brighter than the room add to it.
  • It is temporary: there is no good evidence that screens cause permanent eye damage in adults.

Set up your gaming station

ItemBest practice
DistanceAbout an arm's length (50 to 70 cm) for a monitor; further for larger screens
HeightTop of the screen at or a little below eye level, so you look slightly down; this narrows the eye opening and reduces dryness
Brightness and contrastMatch the screen to the room; avoid gaming in a dark room with a bright screen; add bias lighting behind the monitor
GlareNo window or lamp directly behind or in front of the screen; use a matte screen or anti-reflective coating on glasses
Text and HUD sizeIncrease scaling so small text can be read without leaning in
Refresh rate and flickerHigher refresh rates feel smoother; if you are sensitive to flicker, choose monitors marketed as flicker-free
AirAvoid fans or air conditioning blowing on your face; a humidifier helps in dry climates

Habits that help

  • 20-20-20: every 20 minutes, look about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds. Between matches, take a longer break away from the screen.
  • Blink on purpose: a few slow, complete blinks (squeeze gently) during loading screens.
  • Lubricant drops: preservative-free artificial tears before and during long sessions if your eyes feel dry. Avoid "redness-relief" drops.
  • Sleep: late-night sessions and bright screens delay sleep; use night mode and stop an hour before bed when you can.
  • Hydration and caffeine: heavy caffeine and energy drinks can worsen eyelid twitching and sleep.

Glasses, contact lenses and blue light

  • Even small uncorrected errors, especially astigmatism or early presbyopia (from the early 40s), cause strain. Have an eye test every 1 to 2 years.
  • Esports players need sharp vision for the screen distance: tell your optometrist your setup. Some people benefit from a separate pair for the screen.
  • Contact lens wearers get drier during long sessions; daily disposables and regular breaks help.
  • Blue-light glasses: a 2023 Cochrane review found no evidence that blue-light-filtering lenses reduce eye strain in the short term, and there is no evidence that screen blue light damages the retina. They are not harmful if you like them, but they are not needed.

VR and AR headsets

  • Set the lens distance (IPD) to match your eyes; a wrong setting causes strain and double images.
  • Motion sickness, eye strain and headache are common in the first sessions; build up gradually.
  • Many manufacturers advise against use by young children (often under 10 to 13 years); follow the age rating and limit session length.
  • Use glasses spacers or prescription lens inserts rather than pressing glasses against the headset lenses.

Children, teenagers and myopia

Myopia (short-sightedness) is increasing worldwide. Long periods of near work and too little time outdoors both raise the risk. Gaming itself is not the only cause, but children who game a lot should still get about 2 hours outdoors every day, take breaks, and have yearly eye tests. If a child is becoming more myopic, ask about myopia control: see myopia control in 2026.

See an eye doctor if you have: double vision; an eye that turns in or out; headaches that persist after breaks; sudden blur, flashes or floaters; or a painful red eye. Photosensitive epilepsy: flashing images can trigger seizures in a small number of people; stop and seek medical advice after any episode of jerking, blank spells or loss of awareness while gaming.
