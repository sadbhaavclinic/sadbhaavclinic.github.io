Spectacle Lens Materials and Coatings: CR-39, Polycarbonate, Trivex, High Index, Abbe Value and Blue-Cut

Last updated: November 2026

RPC Rounds for Optometrists · B.Optom study guide

Choosing the lens material is one of the most important dispensing decisions: it decides thickness, weight, safety, optical quality and price. Learn the four numbers for each material (refractive index, Abbe value, density and impact resistance) and you can explain every recommendation to a patient.

Comparing lens materials

MaterialRefractive indexAbbe valueDensity (g/cm³)Notes
Crown glass1.523592.54Excellent optics and scratch resistance; heavy; shatters; rarely used now
CR-39 (allyl diglycol carbonate)1.498581.32Standard plastic: light, good optics, cheap; scratches without a hard coat
Mid-index plastics1.53–1.5636–45about 1.2–1.3Slightly thinner than CR-39
Trivex1.5343–451.11Lightest; impact resistant like polycarbonate with better optics; good for rimless and children
Polycarbonate1.586301.20Very impact resistant (children, sports, safety, one-eyed patients); blocks UV; lower Abbe gives colour fringes
High-index 1.601.60about 36–42about 1.3Thinner for moderate powers
High-index 1.671.67about 32about 1.35Thinner for higher powers
High-index 1.741.74about 33about 1.47Thinnest plastic; high powers
High-index glass1.7–1.9about 30–40up to about 4.0Very thin but heavy
  • Refractive index: higher index bends light more, so the same power needs flatter curves and a thinner lens.
  • Abbe value (constringence): the higher the number, the less chromatic aberration. Values under about 40 can cause colour fringes when looking off-axis, especially in high powers; good centration helps.
  • Reflection rises with index (about 4% per surface for CR-39, more for 1.74), so high-index lenses need an anti-reflection coating.
  • Impact resistance: polycarbonate and Trivex are the materials of choice for children, sports, safety eyewear and anyone with one useful eye.

Coatings and treatments

CoatingWhat it doesAdvice
Hard (scratch-resistant) coatProtects plastic surfacesEssential on CR-39, polycarbonate and high index
Anti-reflection (AR, multicoat)Destructive interference reduces reflections to under about 1%Reduces ghost images and glare (night driving, screens); improves appearance; clean with a microfibre cloth
Hydrophobic and oleophobic top coatsRepel water and greaseEasier cleaning
UV protectionBlocks ultraviolet up to about 380–400 nmPolycarbonate, Trivex and most high-index lenses block UV inherently
PhotochromicDarkens in UV light, clears indoorsSlower to clear in heat; activate less behind car windscreens (UV is blocked)
Tints and polarised lensesReduce brightness; polarisers cut reflected glarePolarised lenses help driving and water sports; tints over about 75–80% absorption are unsuitable for driving, especially at night
Mirror coatingsReflect light for comfort and appearanceCosmetic and outdoor use
Blue-light filtering ("blue-cut")Reduces some short-wavelength visible lightA 2023 Cochrane review found no clear benefit for eye strain, sleep or eye health; do not oversell

Choosing the material: a quick guide

PatientSuggested material
Child, sports, one-eyed patient, safety usePolycarbonate or Trivex
Rimless or drill-mount framesTrivex or polycarbonate (resist cracking at drill holes)
Low power (up to about ±2.00 D)CR-39 with hard and AR coats
Moderate power (±2 to ±4 D)1.60 index
High power (over ±4 to ±6 D)1.67 or 1.74 with AR coat and a small frame
Sensitive to colour fringesHigher Abbe material (CR-39, Trivex) and accurate centration

For lens designs (aspheric, bifocal, progressive) see spectacle lens designs.

Practical pearls

  • When a patient complains of colour fringes with new high-index lenses, check the centration and fitting height before blaming the material.
  • Recommend AR coating with every high-index lens: reflections increase with index and spoil the thinner look.
  • For a child with one good eye, polycarbonate or Trivex is not optional: it is a safety requirement.

Where students slip

  • Saying high index always means lighter: high-index glass is heavy.
  • Overselling blue-cut lenses as protection against eye disease.
  • Using polycarbonate for a patient bothered by off-axis colour fringes without explaining the Abbe trade-off.
