Keratorefractive surgical procedures (by mechanism and depth)

Every corneal refractive procedure changes corneal curvature by adding tissue, removing tissue, relaxing it, or shrinking (coagulating or compressing) it, at one of three depths.

Blepharitis

TypeFeatures
AnteriorLash base: staphylococcal (collarettes, madarosis) or seborrhoeic (greasy scales); Demodex gives cylindrical dandruff
Posterior (meibomian gland dysfunction)Capped or inspissated glands, frothy tears, lid margin telangiectasia; often with rosacea

Scleritis (Watson and Hayreh)

TypeFeatures
Anterior diffuseWidespread inflammation; the commonest and mildest
Anterior nodularImmobile, tender scleral nodule
Anterior necrotising with inflammationSevere pain, avascular patches; strongly linked to systemic vasculitis (rheumatoid arthritis, GPA)
Anterior necrotising without inflammation (scleromalacia perforans)Painless thinning in long-standing rheumatoid arthritis

Episcleritis is separate: simple (sectoral or diffuse) or nodular, blanches with phenylephrine and is rarely sight-threatening.

Primary angle-closure disease (ISGEO, updated)

StageDefinition
Primary angle-closure suspect (PACS)Iridotrabecular contact for at least 180° (originally 270°) on gonioscopy; normal IOP, no PAS, no optic neuropathy
Primary angle closure (PAC)PACS plus raised IOP and/or peripheral anterior synechiae; no glaucomatous optic neuropathy
Primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG)PAC plus glaucomatous optic neuropathy and field loss
Acute angle closureA symptomatic attack (pain, haloes, high IOP, mid-dilated pupil) at any stage

ZAP and ANA-LIG showed most PACS eyes do not progress, so prophylactic iridotomy is no longer automatic for PACS.

Spaeth gonioscopic classification

ComponentGrades
Iris insertionA: anterior to Schwalbe line; B: behind Schwalbe line (at the trabecular meshwork); C: at the scleral spur; D: deep (ciliary body band); E: extremely deep

Mechanical eye injury: Birmingham Eye Trauma Terminology (BETT)

TypeSubtypeDefinition
Closed globeContusionNo full-thickness wound; injury from direct blow or shape change
Lamellar lacerationPartial-thickness wound of the eye wall

Penetrating injurySingle entrance wound from a sharp object
Intraocular foreign bodyRetained foreign body with an entrance wound
Perforating injuryEntrance and exit wounds

Intraocular foreign bodies

BasisCategories
LocationAnterior chamber or angle, iris, lens, vitreous, retina or choroid, intraorbital (outside the globe)
Material: inertGlass, plastic, stone, gold, silver, platinum (may be left if quiet and away from the visual axis)
Material: toxicIron and steel (siderosis), copper (chalcosis; pure copper causes acute sterile endophthalmitis), lead, zinc
Material: organicWood and vegetable matter: high infection risk (fungi, Bacillus)
Magnetic propertiesMagnetic (iron, steel) or non-magnetic; avoid MRI until a metallic IOFB is excluded

Image with CT (not MRI). Remove toxic and organic foreign bodies early.
