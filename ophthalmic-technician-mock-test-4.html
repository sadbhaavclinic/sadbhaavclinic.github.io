Ophthalmic Technician Mock Test 4: Ophthalmic Instruments and Investigations (40 MCQs with Answers)

For: Optometrists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • 40 original questions on slit lamp illuminations, ophthalmoscopes, tonometers, biometry, perimetry, OCT, angiography, B-scan, topography, pachymetry, specular microscopy and tear tests.
  • Answer all of them, then press "Check my score": correct answers turn green and wrong ones red.
  • Open "Show answer" for the explanation.
  • Then try the other mock tests in the series.

Set a timer for 40 minutes. The option order is mixed, so read every choice carefully.

1. Slit lamp illumination used to see corneal opacities against the red reflex is:

Show answer

Answer: Retro-illumination. Light reflected from the iris or fundus back-lights the cornea.

2. Specular reflection on the slit lamp is used to view the:

Show answer

Answer: Corneal endothelium. The endothelial mosaic is seen in the zone of specular reflection.

3. The van Herick technique estimates:

Show answer

Answer: Peripheral anterior chamber depth. Compare peripheral chamber depth with corneal thickness at the limbus.

4. Gonioscopy is needed because the angle cannot be seen directly due to:

Show answer

Answer: Total internal reflection at the cornea. A goniolens overcomes total internal reflection.

5. The indirect ophthalmoscope gives an image that is:

Show answer

Answer: Inverted and real. Wide field, about 2–3× magnification with a 20 D lens.

6. The direct ophthalmoscope magnifies about:

Show answer

Answer: 15×. Narrow field, upright image.

7. NCT (air-puff) tonometry is useful for:

Show answer

Answer: Screening, without anaesthetic. Confirm high or unusual readings with Goldmann.

8. Rebound tonometry (iCare) is popular in children because:

Show answer

Answer: It needs no anaesthetic and is quick. Light probe touches the cornea briefly.

9. The Schiotz tonometer is an:

Show answer

Answer: Indentation tonometer. It uses weights and conversion tables; affected by scleral rigidity.

10. Before Goldmann tonometry, the prism should be:

Show answer

Answer: Disinfected and dried as per protocol. Prevents transmission of infection.

11. Optical biometry (e.g. IOLMaster) may fail in:

Show answer

Answer: Dense or white cataract. Use ultrasound A-scan when the optical signal is poor.

12. In contact A-scan, pressing too hard on the cornea gives an axial length that is:

Show answer

Answer: Too short. Corneal compression shortens the measured axial length.

13. Keratometry values (K) are needed for IOL calculation because they give the:

Show answer

Answer: Corneal power. Errors in K readings also cause refractive surprise.

14. A good A-scan of a phakic eye shows spikes from:

Show answer

Answer: Cornea, anterior and posterior lens surfaces, retina and sclera. Retinal spike should be tall and steeply rising.

15. The HFA 10-2 programme is used to test:

Show answer

Answer: Central 10°, e.g. advanced glaucoma and hydroxychloroquine screening. It tests points within 10° of fixation.

16. High false positives on a visual field mean the patient:

Show answer

Answer: Pressed the button when no stimulus was shown. Trigger-happy patients; results look better than reality.

17. Mean deviation (MD) on a field printout indicates:

Show answer

Answer: Overall average depression of the field. PSD shows localised irregularity.

18. OCT RNFL thickness is used to monitor:

Show answer

Answer: Glaucoma. Thinning shows nerve fibre loss.

19. OCT of the macula is the key test for:

Show answer

Answer: Diabetic macular oedema and wet AMD treatment decisions. It measures central thickness and fluid.

20. Fundus fluorescein angiography uses dye injected into a:

Show answer

Answer: Vein, usually in the arm. Photographs are taken as dye passes through retinal vessels.

21. After FFA, patients should be told their urine and skin may:

Show answer

Answer: Turn yellow-orange for a day or so. Harmless discolouration.

22. B-scan ultrasound is used when:

Show answer

Answer: The view of the retina is blocked by dense cataract or haemorrhage. It shows retinal detachment, masses and vitreous haemorrhage.

23. Corneal topography is essential before:

Show answer

Answer: Refractive surgery and to detect keratoconus. It maps the curvature of the whole cornea.

24. Pachymetry measures:

Show answer

Answer: Corneal thickness. Ultrasound or optical; needed for glaucoma and refractive surgery.

25. Specular microscopy before cataract surgery is useful in:

Show answer

Answer: Fuchs dystrophy or previous corneal surgery. Low cell counts raise the risk of corneal decompensation.

26. Schirmer test without anaesthetic measures:

Show answer

Answer: Basal plus reflex tear secretion. Wetting under about 5 mm in 5 minutes suggests severe deficiency.

27. Tear break-up time under about 10 seconds suggests:

Show answer

Answer: Unstable tear film. Measured with fluorescein after a blink.

28. Exophthalmometry (Hertel) measures:

Show answer

Answer: Forward protrusion of the eye. Useful in thyroid eye disease.

29. The cover–uncover test detects:

Show answer

Answer: Latent and manifest squint (tropia and phoria). The alternate cover test shows the total deviation.

30. The Worth four-dot test assesses:

Show answer

Answer: Fusion and suppression. Uses red–green glasses.

31. Stereopsis can be tested with:

Show answer

Answer: TNO or Titmus fly test. Measures depth perception in seconds of arc.

32. Contrast sensitivity is tested with:

Show answer

Answer: Pelli-Robson chart. Patients can have good acuity but poor contrast, e.g. in cataract.

33. An autorefractor reading should be:

Show answer

Answer: Refined with subjective refraction. Machines overestimate myopia in young people who accommodate.

34. Fundus photography is used to:

Show answer

Answer: Document and compare retinal findings over time. Also used in diabetic screening programmes.

35. Calibration of a Goldmann tonometer is checked with a:

Show answer

Answer: Calibration bar (weights) at 0, 2 and 6 positions. Check regularly and log the result.

36. Retinal photography with a non-mydriatic camera means:

Show answer

Answer: Images can often be taken without dilating drops. Small pupils may still need dilation.

37. An ERG (electroretinogram) tests the function of the:

Show answer

Answer: Retina. Used in inherited retinal disease.

38. Visual evoked potential (VEP) tests the:

Show answer

Answer: Visual pathway up to the cortex. Delayed in optic neuritis.

39. The Placido disc assesses:

Show answer

Answer: Corneal surface regularity. Distorted rings suggest irregular astigmatism.

40. Before an OCT scan, the most important patient instruction is to:

Show answer

Answer: Keep looking at the fixation target and blink between scans. Movement and dry cornea reduce signal quality.
