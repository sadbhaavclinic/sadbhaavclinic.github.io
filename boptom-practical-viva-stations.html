B.Optom Practical and Viva Stations: 30 OSCE-Style Stations With Model Answers

Last updated: November 2026

RPC Rounds for Optometrists · B.Optom study guide

Practical exams in optometry test what you do with the patient and how you explain it. These 30 stations follow the B.Optom practical and viva pattern: a task, the questions examiners usually ask, model answers and a tip on what earns marks. Open a station, attempt the questions aloud, then read the answers.

How to use: practise in pairs. One student acts as examiner and reads the task and questions; the other performs the skill and answers in 5–7 minutes. Swap roles. Revise the full technique on the linked topic pages from the curriculum.

Groups: Refraction · Instruments · Binocular vision · Contact lenses · Dispensing · Low vision · Ocular disease

Refraction

Station 1: Visual acuity: distance and near

Task: Measure and record distance and near visual acuity in both eyes.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. How do you record acuity on a 6 m Snellen chart if the patient reads the 6/9 line with two mistakes?
    Show answer
    6/9 −2 (or 6/12 if your clinic records the last complete line). Always state the chart and distance.
  2. What if the patient cannot read the top letter?
    Show answer
    Move the patient closer (e.g. 3/60), then counting fingers, hand movements, perception of light with projection.
  3. Why use a pinhole?
    Show answer
    To tell refractive error from other causes: improvement with pinhole suggests uncorrected refractive error.
  4. Why is logMAR preferred in research?
    Show answer
    Equal steps between lines, five letters per line, each letter scored.

Examiner tip: Occlude properly (palm or occluder, not fingers pressing the eye) and test the worse eye first only if your protocol says so.

Station 2: Retinoscopy

Task: Perform streak retinoscopy and give your gross and net findings.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. What does "with" movement mean?
    Show answer
    The reflex moves in the same direction as the streak: the far point is behind you (hyperopia, low myopia or under-neutralised). Add plus.
  2. How do you find the axis in astigmatism?
    Show answer
    Look for the break, the width and the skew of the reflex; rotate the streak until it aligns with the reflex.
  3. Working distance correction at 67 cm?
    Show answer
    −1.50 D (at 50 cm, −2.00 D).
  4. A scissors reflex suggests?
    Show answer
    Keratoconus or irregular cornea.

Examiner tip: Keep your working distance constant and the patient fixating the distance target with the other eye fogged.

Station 3: Subjective refraction and cross-cylinder

Task: Refine sphere and cylinder after retinoscopy.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. What is the end point of the sphere?
    Show answer
    Maximum plus for best visual acuity (MPMVA).
  2. Which do you refine first with the cross-cylinder?
    Show answer
    Axis first, then power.
  3. How do you keep the spherical equivalent constant?
    Show answer
    Add +0.25 D sphere for every −0.50 D cylinder added.
  4. How do you check binocular balance?
    Show answer
    Prism dissociation (Humphriss or 3Δ up/down) or alternate occlusion, then balance with +0.25 D steps.

Examiner tip: Say what you are doing to the patient in plain words; examiners mark communication.

Station 4: Duochrome test

Task: Use the duochrome to check the sphere end point.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Green clearer: what do you do?
    Show answer
    Add plus (too much minus).
  2. Red clearer?
    Show answer
    Add minus (too much plus).
  3. Why does it work in colour-blind patients?
    Show answer
    It depends on chromatic aberration, not colour naming.
  4. Ideal end point?
    Show answer
    Equal, or red very slightly clearer.

Examiner tip: Do it monocularly with the room lights dimmed.

Station 5: Near add and amplitude of accommodation

Task: Determine a near add for a 50-year-old.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. How do you measure amplitude?
    Show answer
    Push-up (RAF rule) or minus lens method, monocularly with distance correction.
  2. Hofstetter minimum expected amplitude?
    Show answer
    15 − 0.25 × age.
  3. How do you set the add?
    Show answer
    Leave half (or one-third) of the amplitude in reserve; check the range of clear vision at the working distance.
  4. What else confirms it?
    Show answer
    Fused cross-cylinder or NRA/PRA balance.

Examiner tip: Always ask the patient's working distance before you finalise the add.

Station 6: Cycloplegic refraction in a child

Task: Explain how you would refract a 5-year-old with suspected hyperopia.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Which drug and how many drops?
    Show answer
    Cyclopentolate 1% (0.5% under 1 year), one drop twice 5–10 minutes apart; refract after 30–45 minutes.
  2. What do you check before instilling?
    Show answer
    Anterior chamber depth, history of seizures or heart disease, allergies.
  3. What do you prescribe in accommodative esotropia?
    Show answer
    Full cycloplegic correction.
  4. Side effects to warn about?
    Show answer
    Blurred near vision, light sensitivity, rarely flushing, fever or drowsiness.

Examiner tip: Mention the tonus allowance only if you prescribe less than the full correction in a child without esotropia.

Instruments

Station 7: Lensometer

Task: Read and record the power of a pair of glasses.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. First step?
    Show answer
    Focus the eyepiece for your own eye.
  2. How do you find the axis in a minus-cylinder reading?
    Show answer
    Focus the most plus meridian (sphere lines), note the axis, then focus the cylinder lines.
  3. How do you mark the optical centre?
    Show answer
    Centre the target and use the marking pins.
  4. How do you read prism?
    Show answer
    Note how far and in which direction the target is displaced from the centre.

Examiner tip: Read the right lens first with the frame bottom against the table.

Station 8: Keratometry

Task: Measure corneal curvature.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. What does the instrument measure?
    Show answer
    Radius of curvature of the central 3 mm of the anterior cornea.
  2. Which refractive index converts radius to power?
    Show answer
    1.3375.
  3. With-the-rule astigmatism?
    Show answer
    Vertical meridian steeper.
  4. Uses?
    Show answer
    Contact lens fitting, IOL power calculation, monitoring keratoconus.

Examiner tip: Calibrate with a steel ball if asked; tell the patient to blink before you read.

Station 9: Slit lamp illumination techniques

Task: Demonstrate diffuse, direct focal and retro-illumination.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Which shows corneal endothelium?
    Show answer
    Specular reflection.
  2. Which shows corneal oedema at the limbus?
    Show answer
    Sclerotic scatter.
  3. How do you grade cells in the anterior chamber?
    Show answer
    1 mm × 1 mm high-magnification beam, count cells (SUN grading).
  4. Van Herick technique?
    Show answer
    Narrow beam at the temporal limbus, angle 60°; compare peripheral chamber depth with corneal thickness.

Examiner tip: Start low magnification, then increase; adjust the eyepieces and chin rest before the patient sits.

Station 10: Tonometry

Task: Measure IOP with a Goldmann tonometer.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Area flattened?
    Show answer
    3.06 mm diameter.
  2. End point?
    Show answer
    Inner edges of the two semicircles just touching.
  3. Errors?
    Show answer
    Thick mires (high reading), thin cornea or after LASIK (low), astigmatism over 3 D (rotate the prism).
  4. How do you disinfect the prism?
    Show answer
    As per clinic protocol, e.g. hydrogen peroxide or alcohol wipe, or disposable tips.

Examiner tip: Ask about allergy to anaesthetic drops and remove contact lenses first.

Station 11: Direct and indirect ophthalmoscopy

Task: Examine the fundus and describe the optic disc.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. What do you describe at the disc?
    Show answer
    Size, colour, margins, cup-to-disc ratio, rim (ISNT rule), vessels, haemorrhages.
  2. Image in direct vs indirect ophthalmoscopy?
    Show answer
    Direct: erect, about 15× magnification, small field. Indirect: inverted, laterally reversed, larger field.
  3. Which lens for the slit lamp?
    Show answer
    +90 D or +78 D.
  4. Red flags needing same-day referral?
    Show answer
    Disc swelling, new retinal haemorrhages with sudden vision loss, retinal tear or detachment.

Examiner tip: Dim the room, use the patient's right eye with your right eye, and keep the patient fixating a distant target.

Station 12: Visual fields by confrontation and Amsler grid

Task: Screen the visual fields.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. How do you do confrontation?
    Show answer
    Sit 1 m away, eyes level, patient covers one eye, test each quadrant with finger counting or a red target.
  2. What does the Amsler grid test?
    Show answer
    The central 10° (20° × 20° grid at 30 cm): metamorphopsia and scotoma.
  3. Pattern in pituitary tumour?
    Show answer
    Bitemporal hemianopia.
  4. When do you refer for formal perimetry?
    Show answer
    Any defect, glaucoma suspects, neurological symptoms.

Examiner tip: Keep your own eye open opposite the patient's open eye so you compare fields.

Binocular vision

Station 13: Cover test

Task: Perform the cover–uncover and alternate cover test.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. What does cover–uncover detect?
    Show answer
    Tropia (cover) and phoria (uncover).
  2. Alternate cover test?
    Show answer
    Total deviation (phoria plus tropia).
  3. How do you measure the angle?
    Show answer
    Prism cover test: prism base opposite the deviation until no movement.
  4. Distance and near?
    Show answer
    Test at 6 m and 33 cm with an accommodative target.

Examiner tip: Keep the occluder in place 2–3 seconds and watch the uncovered eye first.

Station 14: Near point of convergence and vergences

Task: Measure NPC and fusional vergences.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Normal NPC?
    Show answer
    Under about 6–10 cm (break).
  2. How do you measure vergences?
    Show answer
    Prism bar or rotary prism: blur, break and recovery for base-out and base-in.
  3. Sheard's criterion?
    Show answer
    Compensating vergence (blur) should be at least twice the phoria.
  4. Convergence insufficiency signs?
    Show answer
    Exophoria greater at near, remote NPC, low base-out at near.

Examiner tip: Use an accommodative target and repeat NPC several times to check fatigue.

Station 15: Worth four-dot and stereopsis

Task: Test for fusion, suppression and stereoacuity.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Four lights seen?
    Show answer
    Fusion (or harmonious ARC with a tropia).
  2. Five lights?
    Show answer
    Diplopia.
  3. Two red or three green?
    Show answer
    Suppression of the eye behind the green or red filter respectively.
  4. Normal stereoacuity?
    Show answer
    About 40–60 seconds of arc.

Examiner tip: Check which filter is over which eye before you interpret.

Station 16: Ocular motility and Hess chart

Task: Examine eye movements and interpret a Hess chart.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Positions tested?
    Show answer
    Nine positions of gaze in an H pattern.
  2. Hess chart: smaller field?
    Show answer
    Eye with the underacting muscle.
  3. Sixth nerve palsy?
    Show answer
    Limited abduction, esotropia worse on looking to the affected side.
  4. Fourth nerve palsy?
    Show answer
    Hypertropia worse on looking down and to the opposite side and on head tilt to the same side.

Examiner tip: Ask about double vision in each position.

Station 17: Colour vision

Task: Test colour vision with Ishihara plates.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Test distance and lighting?
    Show answer
    75 cm, daylight or equivalent, 3 seconds per plate.
  2. What does Ishihara detect?
    Show answer
    Red–green defects; not blue–yellow.
  3. Which test grades severity?
    Show answer
    Farnsworth D-15 or 100-hue.
  4. Inheritance of red–green defect?
    Show answer
    X-linked recessive (about 8% of men).

Examiner tip: Test each eye separately when looking for acquired defects.

Contact lenses

Station 18: Soft contact lens fitting

Task: Assess the fit of a soft lens.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Signs of a good fit?
    Show answer
    Centred, full corneal cover, 0.2–0.4 mm movement on blink, easy push-up recovery.
  2. Tight lens?
    Show answer
    Little movement, conjunctival indentation, vision fluctuates or improves after blink.
  3. Loose lens?
    Show answer
    Decentration, excessive movement, edge stand-off, unstable vision.
  4. How do you change the fit?
    Show answer
    Steeper or larger lens to tighten; flatter or smaller to loosen.

Examiner tip: Wait 10–20 minutes after insertion before assessing.

Station 19: RGP fluorescein pattern

Task: Interpret a fluorescein pattern.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Alignment fit?
    Show answer
    Even thin fluorescein under the lens with an edge band.
  2. Steep (apical clearance)?
    Show answer
    Central pooling, mid-peripheral touch, air bubble.
  3. Flat (apical touch)?
    Show answer
    Central dark area, wide peripheral pooling, lens decentres.
  4. With-the-rule toric cornea?
    Show answer
    Dumbbell pattern with touch horizontally.

Examiner tip: Use the cobalt blue filter with a yellow (Wratten) filter if available.

Station 20: Toric soft lens rotation

Task: The lens mark has rotated. Decide what to order.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. LARS rule?
    Show answer
    Left add, right subtract (as you see the mark).
  2. Lens rotated 10° to your right, axis 180?
    Show answer
    Order axis 170.
  3. When to wait?
    Show answer
    If rotation is unstable from blink to blink, re-check fit first.
  4. If over-refraction gives cylinder?
    Show answer
    Use a cross-cylinder calculation (e.g. the contact lens calculator).

Examiner tip: Confirm the mark position at 6 o'clock in primary gaze.

Station 21: Contact lens complications

Task: Identify and manage common complications.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Red painful eye with a corneal infiltrate in a lens wearer?
    Show answer
    Treat as microbial keratitis: stop the lens, same-day referral.
  2. Giant papillary conjunctivitis?
    Show answer
    Large papillae on the upper tarsus, mucus, lens intolerance; change to daily disposables, treat, refit.
  3. Corneal neovascularisation?
    Show answer
    Chronic hypoxia; refit with higher Dk/t (silicone hydrogel) or reduce wearing time.
  4. Acanthamoeba risk factors?
    Show answer
    Tap water, swimming in lenses, poor case hygiene.

Examiner tip: Always ask about sleeping in lenses and water exposure.

Dispensing

Station 22: PD and fitting height

Task: Measure PD and fitting height for progressives.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Which PD for progressives?
    Show answer
    Monocular PD to each pupil centre.
  2. Fitting height measured from?
    Show answer
    Pupil centre to the lowest point of the lens rim, frame adjusted on the face.
  3. Near PD at 40 cm?
    Show answer
    About 3 mm less than distance PD.
  4. Common error?
    Show answer
    Measuring in an unadjusted frame.

Examiner tip: Sit at the patient's eye level and close your right eye to measure their left.

Station 23: Lens and frame selection

Task: Recommend a lens and frame for a −7.00 D myope.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Material?
    Show answer
    High index (1.67 or 1.74) with AR coating, or polycarbonate.
  2. Frame?
    Show answer
    Small, round or oval, PD close to frame PD, full rim.
  3. Why?
    Show answer
    Thinner edges, less weight and less prism from decentration.
  4. What to avoid?
    Show answer
    Large rimless frames.

Examiner tip: Explain the trade-offs: price, Abbe value and appearance.

Station 24: Transposition and spherical equivalent

Task: Transpose a prescription and give its spherical equivalent.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Transpose +1.00/−2.00 × 90.
    Show answer
    −1.00/+2.00 × 180.
  2. Spherical equivalent of −2.00/−1.00 × 180?
    Show answer
    −2.50 D.
  3. Near-only prescription for −1.00/−0.50 × 180, add +2.00?
    Show answer
    +1.00/−0.50 × 180.
  4. Vertex correction needed above?
    Show answer
    About ±4.00 D.

Examiner tip: Write each step; examiners mark the method.

Station 25: Prentice's rule and decentration

Task: Calculate prism from decentration.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Formula?
    Show answer
    Prism (Δ) = decentration (cm) × power (D).
  2. +5.00 D lens decentred 4 mm out?
    Show answer
    2Δ base out.
  3. Where is the base in a minus lens decentred outwards?
    Show answer
    Base in (minus lenses: base opposite to decentration).
  4. Tolerance for horizontal prism (ANSI)?
    Show answer
    About 0.67Δ total.

Examiner tip: Draw the lens and the direction of decentration.

Low vision

Station 26: Low vision assessment

Task: Assess a patient with 6/60 vision due to macular degeneration.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Starting near add (Kestenbaum)?
    Show answer
    Reciprocal of the acuity: 60/6 = +10.00 D.
  2. Which aid for spot reading?
    Show answer
    Hand or stand magnifier, electronic magnifier.
  3. Which aid for television?
    Show answer
    Galilean or Keplerian telescope.
  4. Non-optical aids?
    Show answer
    Lighting, contrast, large print, apps, typoscope.

Examiner tip: Ask about the patient's goals first: reading, faces, mobility.

Station 27: Visual disability certification

Task: Explain how visual disability is graded in India.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Which law?
    Show answer
    Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016.
  2. Based on?
    Show answer
    Best-corrected acuity in each eye and visual fields.
  3. Who certifies?
    Show answer
    A medical board including an ophthalmologist.
  4. Tool?
    Show answer
    The visual disability calculator on this site.

Examiner tip: Optometrists help with accurate acuity and fields; the board certifies.

Ocular disease

Station 28: Dry eye assessment

Task: Assess a patient with dry eye symptoms.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Tests?
    Show answer
    Symptom questionnaire (OSDI), TBUT, fluorescein and lissamine staining, Schirmer, meibomian gland expression.
  2. Abnormal TBUT?
    Show answer
    Under 10 seconds.
  3. First-line management?
    Show answer
    Lid hygiene, warm compresses, preservative-free lubricants, screen breaks.
  4. When to refer?
    Show answer
    Severe staining, filaments, systemic disease (Sjögren), no improvement.

Examiner tip: Do TBUT before any drops or contact tonometry.

Station 29: Red eye triage

Task: Decide whether a red eye needs urgent referral.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. Red flags?
    Show answer
    Pain, reduced vision, photophobia, corneal opacity, irregular pupil, raised IOP, contact lens wearer, trauma.
  2. Conjunctivitis features?
    Show answer
    Discharge, normal vision, no photophobia, follicles or papillae.
  3. Acute angle closure?
    Show answer
    Pain, haloes, mid-dilated pupil, hazy cornea, high IOP: same day.
  4. Chemical injury?
    Show answer
    Irrigate immediately, then refer.

Examiner tip: If in doubt, phone the ophthalmologist; never give steroid drops.

Station 30: Diabetic retinopathy screening

Task: Screen a diabetic patient's fundus.

Examiner questions (answer aloud, then tap to check):

  1. What do you look for?
    Show answer
    Microaneurysms, haemorrhages, exudates, cotton-wool spots, venous beading, IRMA, new vessels, macular thickening.
  2. Severe NPDR (4-2-1 rule)?
    Show answer
    Haemorrhages in 4 quadrants, venous beading in 2, IRMA in 1.
  3. Referral urgency for new vessels?
    Show answer
    Urgent (within days).
  4. Follow-up with no retinopathy?
    Show answer
    Yearly.

Examiner tip: Dilate unless contraindicated and examine the macula carefully.

What examiners mark in every station

  • Greeting, introduction, consent and hand hygiene.
  • Positioning the patient and instrument correctly.
  • A logical sequence, done confidently.
  • Clear explanation to the patient in plain words.
  • Correct recording with units, eye and method.
  • Knowing when to refer.

Practical pearls

  • Say each step aloud as you do it; silent technique loses communication marks.
  • Always record the eye, the method and the units (e.g. "R 6/9, Snellen, 6 m, with glasses").
  • End each station by saying what you would tell the patient and whether you would refer.

Where students slip

  • Forgetting to focus the lensometer eyepiece or dim the room for retinoscopy.
  • Using binocular PD for progressives.
  • Reading the Worth four-dot result without checking which filter is over which eye.
