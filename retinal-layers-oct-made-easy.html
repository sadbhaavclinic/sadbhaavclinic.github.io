Retinal Layers and OCT Made Easy: Where Every Lesion Sits

Last updated: October 2026

Ten retinal layers, four outer OCT bands and a dozen lesion types: it is easy to get lost. But the retina is simply a three-neuron relay with two blood supplies. Once you see that, you can place any haemorrhage, exudate or fluid pocket in its layer, and read an OCT scan from top to bottom.

Four rules

1. Read the relay backwards from the lightLight passes through the whole retina to reach the photoreceptors at the back. The signal then travels forwards: photoreceptor (ONL) → synapse (OPL) → bipolar cell (INL) → synapse (IPL) → ganglion cell (GCL) → axon (NFL) → optic nerve. Three nuclear layers, two plexiform layers.
2. Two blood supplies, split at the OPLThe central retinal artery feeds the inner retina (NFL to INL). The choroid feeds the outer retina (ONL, photoreceptors, RPE). That is why a CRAO whitens the inner retina but leaves a cherry-red spot: the fovea has no inner layers.
3. The shape of a lesion follows the fibresIn the NFL the fibres run along the surface, so blood spreads into flames. In the INL and OPL the cells stand upright, so blood stays as dots and blots. In Henle's layer the fibres radiate from the fovea, so exudate forms a star and cysts look petaloid.
4. On OCT, fibres are bright and nuclei are darkLayers of horizontal fibres or membranes (NFL, IPL, OPL, RPE) reflect strongly; nuclear layers (GCL, INL, ONL) are dark. Fluid is black.

On OCT angiography the retinal capillaries form a superficial plexus (in the GCL/NFL), an intermediate plexus (inner border of the INL) and a deep plexus (outer border of the INL). The choriocapillaris sits just under Bruch's membrane.

The fovea is special

  • Only cones, packed tightly, with the inner layers pushed aside to form the pit; no retinal vessels in the central 0.5 mm (the foveal avascular zone).
  • Its outer layers rely on the choroid, so it stays red in CRAO and swells first in choroidal disease.
  • Xanthophyll (lutein, zeaxanthin) sits in the inner layers here, which is why the macula looks dark on FA and blocks blue light.
  • Foveal hypoplasia (albinism, aniridia) shows as persistent inner layers across the centre on OCT.

Where residents slip

  • Placing hard exudates in the NFL: they sit in the OPL (Henle's layer).
  • Calling all dark spaces on OCT 'CME': check whether it is intraretinal, subretinal or under the RPE.
  • Forgetting the choroid feeds the photoreceptors: it explains the cherry-red spot and why CRAO spares the fovea's colour.
  • Reading the RPE band as the ellipsoid zone: the RPE is the brightest band at the bottom; the EZ is just above it.
  • Mixing up type 1 and type 2 MNV: type 1 is under the RPE, type 2 above it.
