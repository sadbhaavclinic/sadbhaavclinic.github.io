Amblyopia Treatment Updates: Glasses, Patching Doses and Digital Therapy

Last updated: October 2026

Amblyopia management is built on two decades of PEDIG trials, and the core message still stands: glasses first, then the least occlusion that works. What has changed is the arrival of FDA-cleared dichoptic digital therapies that children actually enjoy using. Here is how the old evidence and the new devices fit together.

Step 1: correct the refractive error and wait

  • Prescribe the full cycloplegic correction (adjusted for working distance where appropriate) and give it time.
  • In PEDIG's study of glasses alone in children aged 3–7 with strabismic or anisometropic amblyopia, about 77% improved by 2 or more lines and about 27% resolved with spectacles alone.
  • Improvement can continue for several months; review every 6–8 weeks and start occlusion when acuity plateaus.

Step 2: patching or atropine. Less is often enough.

PEDIG questionAnswer
Moderate amblyopia (20/40–20/80): 2 hours vs 6 hours of daily patching?Similar improvement. Start with 2 hours.
Severe amblyopia (20/100–20/400): 6 hours vs full-time patching?Similar improvement. Start with 6 hours.
Atropine 1% penalisation vs patching for moderate amblyopia?Similar results. Atropine is a good choice when patching fails or is refused.
Weekend atropine vs daily atropine?Similar results.
Is it too late at 7–12 years?No. Older children still respond, especially if not treated before. Response is less consistent at 13–17 years.
What happens after stopping?About one in four children has a recurrence within a year, so wean gradually and keep following up.

Step 3 (or an alternative): dichoptic and binocular therapy

Amblyopia is increasingly understood as a binocular problem, in which the fellow eye actively suppresses the amblyopic eye. Dichoptic therapies present different images to the two eyes to reduce that suppression.

ApproachEvidence
Early tablet games (PEDIG ATS18)A binocular iPad game was less effective than 2 hours of patching, largely because children did not play enough.
Luminopia (virtual-reality headset showing modified TV shows and films)FDA-cleared in 2021 for ages 4–7, extended to ages 8–12 in 2025. RCT (105 children aged 4–7, 1 hour a day, 6 days a week, with glasses): 1.8 lines of improvement vs 0.8 lines with glasses alone at 12 weeks. The trial stopped early for success.
CureSight (eye-tracking glasses that blur the fellow eye's central vision while the child watches videos)FDA-cleared. RCT (103 children aged 4–9): 0.28 logMAR improvement vs 0.23 with 2 hours of daily patching at 16 weeks, which was non-inferior. Adherence was 91% vs 83%.
  • These devices are non-inferior, not superior to patching, and they cost far more. Their real advantage is adherence and acceptability.
  • Most data come from children aged 4–9 with moderate amblyopia and good glasses wear. Evidence in severe or deprivation amblyopia is limited.
  • In India, availability and cost are the main barriers, so patching and atropine remain the backbone of treatment.

Viva pearls

  • Classify amblyopia as strabismic, anisometropic, isoametropic (bilateral refractive) or stimulus deprivation. Deprivation amblyopia needs the most urgent action.
  • Know the starting doses: 2 hours for moderate, 6 hours for severe.
  • Reverse (occlusion) amblyopia is a risk with full-time patching in young children, so check the fellow eye at every visit.
  • Crowding: amblyopic eyes read single optotypes better than lines, so test with crowded charts.
