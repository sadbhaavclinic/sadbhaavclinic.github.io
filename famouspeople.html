Famous People with Eye Disease: What an Ophthalmologist Sees

Last updated: October 2026

Well-known people who lived with blindness or serious eye disease, with the likely diagnosis seen through an ophthalmologist's eyes. Many diagnoses in historical figures are educated guesses, so the confidence is noted.

On this page

  • Artists and writers
  • Scientists and leaders
  • Musicians
  • Sport and achievement

Artists and writers

PersonWhoEye conditionOphthalmology angle
Claude Monet (1840–1926)French Impressionist painterBilateral cataracts; right cataract removed in 1923His late paintings turned muddy and red-brown, then bluer after surgery: a classic lesson on how cataract changes colour perception
Edgar Degas (1834–1917)French painterProgressive central visual loss from mid-life, probably a macular diseaseHis work moved to pastels and coarser strokes as fine detail became impossible
John Milton (1608–1674)English poetBlind by 1652; cause debated (glaucoma or retinal detachment suggested)Dictated Paradise Lost after losing his sight
Homer (c. 8th century BCE)Greek poetTraditionally described as blindLegend rather than recorded history
Surdas (c. 1478–1583)Indian devotional poet and singerTraditionally said to be blind from birthComposed the Sur Sagar; blindness is part of tradition
Jorge Luis Borges (1899–1986)Argentine writerProgressive hereditary visual loss, blind by his mid-50s (his father was affected too)Became director of the National Library of Argentina the year he lost his reading vision
James Thurber (1894–1961)American humorist and cartoonistLost his left eye to an arrow at age 7; the right eye later failedSympathetic ophthalmia is the usual explanation; he drew on huge sheets with thick crayon
Joseph Pulitzer (1847–1911)Newspaper publisherRetinal detachment and progressive visual loss in his 40sRan his newspapers through secretaries who read to him

Scientists and leaders

PersonWhoEye conditionOphthalmology angle
Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)AstronomerBlind by 1638; glaucoma or uveitis suggestedPopular stories blame solar observation; the evidence points more to glaucoma
Leonhard Euler (1707–1783)MathematicianLost the right eye's vision in the 1730s; later cataract in the left eye left him nearly blindProduced about half his work after losing his sight
Joseph Plateau (1801–1883)Belgian physicist (persistence of vision)Blind in later life; he blamed staring at the sun in an experimentSolar retinopathy alone rarely causes complete blindness, so the cause is debated
Horatio Nelson (1758–1805)British admiralLost sight in the right eye from a wound at Calvi (1794)The origin of "turning a blind eye"
Louis Braille (1809–1852)Inventor of brailleInjured one eye with an awl at age 3; the other eye failed soon afterProbably sympathetic ophthalmia or spreading infection; invented his six-dot code at 15
Thomas Gore (1870–1949)US senatorBlinded in both eyes by two separate childhood accidentsThe first blind member of the US Senate
David Paterson (born 1954)Governor of New YorkSevere visual loss after an infant infection affected the optic nervesThe first legally blind US governor

Musicians

PersonWhoEye conditionOphthalmology angle
Stevie Wonder (born 1950)Singer-songwriterRetinopathy of prematurity (born about 6 weeks early)Often cited when teaching about oxygen and ROP
Ray Charles (1930–2004)Singer and pianistLost his sight by age 7, usually attributed to glaucomaA reminder that childhood glaucoma needs early surgery
Andrea Bocelli (born 1958)TenorCongenital glaucoma; became completely blind after a football injury at 12Classic case of congenital glaucoma with poor visual outcome
Ronnie Milsap (born 1943)Country singerCongenital glaucomaBlind from early childhood
Ravindra Jain (1944–2015)Indian music composer and lyricistBlind from birthComposed for Hindi films and television
Doc Watson (1923–2012)Folk guitaristLost his sight before his first birthday from an eye infectionInfective blindness in infancy
Rahsaan Roland Kirk (1935–1977)Jazz multi-instrumentalistBlind shortly after birth (attributed to a medical treatment)Played several saxophones at once

Sport and achievement

PersonWhoEye conditionOphthalmology angle
Helen Keller (1880–1968)Author and activistDeaf-blind after an illness at 19 monthsTaught by Anne Sullivan, who herself had trachoma
Erik Weihenmayer (born 1968)MountaineerJuvenile X-linked retinoschisis; blind by 13First blind person to climb Everest (2001)
Marla Runyan (born 1969)Olympic runnerStargardt diseaseCompeted at the 2000 Sydney Olympics
Srikanth Bolla (born 1991)Indian industrialistBlind from birthFounder of Bollant Industries
Esref Armagan (born 1953)Turkish painterBorn blindPaints perspective and shading by touch: studied by neuroscientists
Abraham Nemeth (1918–2013)MathematicianBorn blindCreated the Nemeth braille code for mathematics

This page was rewritten in October 2026 in our own words. Historical diagnoses are interpretations, not medical records.
