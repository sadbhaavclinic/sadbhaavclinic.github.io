Orthoptics Module 3: The Orthoptic Examination: Cover Tests, Prisms, Motility and Binocular Tests

For: Optometrists

Last updated: November 2026

Key points in 60 seconds

  • Order: history → acuity → observation (head posture, lids) → corneal reflections → cover test near and distance → ocular movements → convergence → prism measurement → binocular function.
  • Cover/uncover detects a tropia; alternate cover reveals the total deviation (tropia + phoria). Prism cover test measures it.
  • Prism dioptres: 1Δ deviates light 1 cm at 1 m; about 2Δ ≈ 1°.
  • Record with a standard notation: e.g. “Near: R ET 20Δ; Distance: R ET 15Δ”.

Practise: Mock 1 · Mock 2 · Mock 3 · Viva and OSCE · Career guide

Exam map: Investigation: history, cover test, prism measurement, motility, convergence and accommodation, sensory tests.

History

Onset and duration, constant or intermittent, diplopia, head posture, family history, birth and development, previous glasses, patching or surgery, general health (thyroid, myasthenia, diabetes, neurology), and the patient’s main concern.

Observation and corneal reflections

  • Abnormal head posture: face turn (horizontal), head tilt (often IV palsy), chin up or down (A/V pattern, vertical palsies, nystagmus null point).
  • Facial asymmetry, ptosis, epicanthus (pseudo-squint).
  • Hirschberg test: each 1 mm decentration of the corneal light reflex ≈ 7° (about 15Δ). Reflex at the pupil margin ≈ 15°, mid-iris ≈ 30°, limbus ≈ 45°.
  • Krimsky test: prisms in front of the fixing eye until the reflexes are centred; used when cover test is impossible.

Cover tests

TestMethodFinding
Cover/uncoverCover one eye; watch the otherMovement of the uncovered eye = tropia (in = exotropia corrected, out = esotropia)
UncoverWatch the covered eye as cover is removedRecovery movement = phoria
Alternate coverMove cover from eye to eye without allowing binocular viewTotal deviation; dissociates phorias
Prism cover testAlternate cover with increasing prism until no movementMeasures total deviation in prism dioptres
  • Test at near (33 cm) with an accommodative target and at distance (6 m), and in some cases far distance (window).
  • Note speed and quality of recovery of a phoria (rapid, slow, or breaks down = decompensating).
  • Prism placement: base opposite to the direction of deviation (base out for eso, base in for exo, base down before a hyper eye).

Ocular movements

  • Versions (both eyes) in the nine positions, then ductions (one eye) if limited.
  • Grade under- and overactions (−4 to +4); note retraction, narrowing of palpebral fissure (Duane), pain on movement, nystagmus, diplopia position.
  • Saccades and pursuits for neurological cases; doll’s head to separate supranuclear from nuclear palsies.
  • Hess or Lees screen: dissociated charts plotting each eye’s position; the smaller field belongs to the paretic eye; shows under/overactions and progress.
  • Field of binocular single vision: plotted on a perimeter; useful for documenting diplopia for driving and surgery.

Convergence and accommodation

  • Near point of convergence (NPC): RAF rule or target; normal break within about 6–10 cm.
  • Near point of accommodation / amplitude: push-up method; minimum amplitude (Hofstetter) = 15 − 0.25 × age.
  • AC/A ratio: heterophoria method or gradient method (change in deviation with +/− lenses); normal about 3–5Δ per dioptre. High AC/A explains convergence excess esotropia.

TestUseInterpretation

Recording

Use clear standard notation: test, distance, with/without glasses, fixing eye, type and size of deviation (e.g. “c Rx, near: L ET 25Δ, dist: L ET 20Δ; alternate fixation”). Draw motility as a nine-position diagram. Write “right” and “left” fully where ambiguity is possible.

Practice questions (short answer and viva)

How do you distinguish a tropia from a phoria?

Cover/uncover: movement of the uncovered eye shows a tropia; uncover/alternate cover shows a phoria that is controlled binocularly.

How is a 30Δ esotropia corrected with prisms?

Base-out prism (split between eyes if large) in the prism cover test.

What is the Hirschberg estimate for a reflex at the pupil margin?

About 15° (roughly 30Δ).

What does a negative 4Δ base-out response suggest?

A central suppression scotoma, as in microtropia.

What is the normal AC/A ratio?

About 3–5Δ per dioptre of accommodation.

Self-test: 8 MCQs

Answer all, then press “Check my score”. Option order is mixed.

1. Alternate cover test measures:

Show answer

Answer: Total deviation (tropia + phoria). Dissociates fully.

2. For exotropia, prism base is placed:

Show answer

Answer: In. Apex toward deviation.

3. Worth four-dot: patient with red glass on right sees 2 red lights. This is:

Show answer

Answer: Suppression of left eye. Only the right (red) eye seen.

4. Bagolini striated glasses are:

Show answer

Answer: Minimally dissociating. Near natural viewing.

5. 1 mm of corneal reflex decentration ≈:

Show answer

Answer: 7°. Hirschberg.

6. Hofstetter minimum amplitude at age 20 is:

Show answer

Answer: 10 D. 15 − 5 = 10 D.

7. The Frisby stereotest uses:

Show answer

Answer: Real depth plates. No glasses needed.

8. On a Hess chart the smaller field belongs to the:

Show answer

Answer: Eye with the underacting (paretic) muscle. Paretic eye.
