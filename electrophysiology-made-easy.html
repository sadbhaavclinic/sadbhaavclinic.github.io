ERG, EOG and VEP Made Easy: One Test per Level

Last updated: October 2026

Electrophysiology reports look like a page of squiggles and numbers. The trick is to remember that each test listens to one level of the visual pathway. Ask which level is abnormal and the diagnosis usually follows.

Four rules

1. One test, one levelEOG = RPE. Full-field ERG = the whole retina (rods and cones). Pattern ERG = macula (P50) and ganglion cells (N95). Multifocal ERG = a map of central cone function. VEP = the whole path to the visual cortex, dominated by the macula.
2. a-wave = photoreceptors, b-wave = the next cellThe negative a-wave comes from photoreceptors; the positive b-wave mainly from ON-bipolar and Müller cells. If the a-wave is normal but the b-wave small (negative ERG), the problem is after the photoreceptors.
3. Separate rods and cones with light and speedIn the dark with a dim flash only rods respond. Background light saturates rods, so light-adapted responses are cones. Rods cannot follow a 30 Hz flicker, so flicker is pure cone.
4. The full-field ERG misses small lesionsA normal full-field ERG does not rule out macular disease: the macula is only a small part of the retina. Use pattern or multifocal ERG for the centre.

Causes of a negative ERG

  • X-linked retinoschisis (with foveal schisis in a boy)
  • Congenital stationary night blindness: complete (ON-bipolar defect, e.g. NYX) and incomplete (CACNA1F)
  • Central retinal artery occlusion or ischaemic CRVO: inner retina dies, photoreceptors (choroid-fed) survive
  • Melanoma-associated retinopathy (antibodies against ON-bipolar cells)
  • Quinine toxicity, juvenile Batten disease, some birdshot chorioretinopathy

Memory hook: Negative ERG: “Schisis, Night blindness, Artery, Melanoma, Quinine” — SNAM-Q.

EOG: the RPE test

  • The resting potential falls in the dark (dark trough) and rises in the light (light peak). The Arden ratio is light peak ÷ dark trough.
  • Normal is roughly 1.8 or more (labs differ); below about 1.5 is clearly abnormal.
  • Best vitelliform macular dystrophy (BEST1): abnormal EOG with a normal ERG, even in carriers with a normal fundus. This is the classic exam pairing.
  • In widespread photoreceptor disease (RP) the EOG is also low, but then the ERG is abnormal too, so the EOG adds little.

Where residents slip

  • Accepting a normal full-field ERG as 'retina normal': the macula can still be diseased.
  • Forgetting the EOG in a child with a vitelliform lesion: Best disease has a low Arden ratio with a normal ERG.
  • Calling a negative ERG 'severe retinal degeneration': the photoreceptors are working; look for schisis, CSNB, CRAO or MAR.
  • Blaming the optic nerve for an abnormal VEP without checking the macula (PERG P50, OCT).
  • Using a pattern VEP in a patient who cannot fix: use a flash VEP for babies and very poor vision.
