Retina Rounds in Unit 1: Pearls, Macular Numbers and Occlusions

Last updated: October 2026

Teaching points from retina rounds in Unit 1 (Prof. Garg's fundology sessions), tidied up and checked against current practice. Where practice has changed since the rounds, the update is noted.

On this page

  • Pearls from the rounds
  • Macular anatomy numbers
  • Vascular occlusion numbers

Pearls from the rounds

PearlExplanation and current note
Heredofamilial macular dystrophy, not "degeneration"Bilateral, symmetrical, in young patients: dull foveal reflex with mottled pigment and a silvery sheen. Look for family history and order OCT, autofluorescence and ERG
Young patient with a vascular occlusionAlways look for vitreous cells: inflammation (vasculitis, Behçet, sarcoid, TB) must be excluded before calling it a simple occlusion
Shafer sign (tobacco dust) is less reliable in some eyesPigment in the anterior vitreous strongly suggests a retinal break, but can also follow uveitis, trauma, intraocular surgery or vitreous haemorrhage
Cylinder and high spheresAbove about 4 D of sphere, small cylinders add little to blur; a 1 D cylinder still matters and should be corrected
High risk of RD before cataract surgeryTreat symptomatic breaks and high-risk lattice (fellow-eye RD, high myopia, Stickler) with laser before surgery
PRP and macular oedemaPRP can worsen DME. Old rule: focal laser before PRP. Current practice: treat centre-involving DME with anti-VEGF first; anti-VEGF also controls PDR (Protocol S), with PRP as needed
Neovascularisation of the angleNVA can occur without visible NVI, especially after CRVO, so do undilated gonioscopy in every ischaemic occlusion
Signs of a long-standing RDPigmented demarcation lines, intraretinal macrocysts, retinal thinning and subretinal bands

Macular anatomy numbers

StructureSizeNotes
Macula (area centralis)About 5.5 mm diameterHistologically: more than one layer of ganglion cells; clinically: xanthophyll
FoveaAbout 1.5 mm diameterCentre lies about 4 mm temporal and 0.8 mm inferior to the disc centre
FoveolaAbout 0.35 mm diameterOnly cones, no ganglion cells
Foveal avascular zoneAbout 0.4–0.5 mm diameterLarger in diabetic maculopathy

Vascular occlusion numbers

ConditionKey facts
CRAOEmboli are found in about 20%; the rest are thrombotic, vasculitic or traumatic. Exclude giant cell arteritis in everyone over 50. Iris neovascularisation in about 15–20%. Refer via the stroke pathway
BRAOAbout 90% embolic; also inflammatory causes (toxoplasmosis, acute retinal necrosis). NVI is very rare
BoxcarringSegmentation of the blood column in arteries and veins distal to an arterial block
BRVORisk factors: hypertension, cardiovascular disease, raised BMI, glaucoma. Fellow eye affected in about 10%. Ischaemic if more than 5 disc areas of non-perfusion; neovascularisation in about 40% of ischaemic BRVO, and about 60% of those bleed
CRVORisk factors: hypertension, diabetes, glaucoma. Ischaemic if 10 or more disc areas of non-perfusion (CVOS), with poor vision, RAPD and extensive haemorrhage
Endophthalmitis after intravitreal triamcinoloneReported up to about 1.4%, including sterile pseudo-endophthalmitis; about 0.6% when those are excluded (older series)
