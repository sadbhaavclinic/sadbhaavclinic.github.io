Eye Health, in Rhyme

On Cataract

The riper the cataract, the harder the cure,

Don't wait for it to fully mature.

It's not a fruit that needs full ripening time,

Waiting too long makes recovery a climb.

Cataract surgery — a one-time affair,

Think twice before choosing “cheapest” with care.

If lights scatter and haze your night drive view,

It's likely cataract — get it checked, it's due.

Done with one eye? Don't wait too long for the next,

A week or two apart keeps recovery in context.

With cataract surgery, glasses too can go,

Multifocal lenses let near and far vision flow.

Not everyone's a fit — that's worth a talk first,

Choose what suits your needs, not just what sounds best.

Glasses cost more than the surgery that's due?

That math never adds up — and shouldn't confuse you.

A cheap lens gets picked, but pricey frames get bought,

While doctors get blamed for the fee that they sought.

Malls and movie tickets drain thousands with ease,

Yet a doctor's honest fee is what makes people freeze.

Every cataract patient, some years down the line,

Gets a cloudy film — it's simply just a matter of time.

A tiny laser opening clears that veil away,

A short, painless fix — nothing to dismay.

When should the surgery actually be done?

If glasses can't sharpen the blur you can't outrun,

If night driving turns lights into a scattered blur,

The time has come — don't let more days occur.

And any season works now, rain or shine or chill,

Cataract surgery waits on no calendar at all.

Today or tomorrow, the surgery's still due,

So why wait and suffer when the outcome stays true?

Numbers keep shifting, glasses changed again and again,

Yet the blur persists — that's the surgery's signal, plain.

Your children are busy, caught up in their own race,

Good vision keeps you independent, holding your place.

A single day's procedure, home again by evening light,

No reason left to fear it — science has taken flight.

"Wait till it's fully ripe" is an outdated old belief,

Even a small cataract today is safely brought to relief.

Operating one eye won't harm the other in kind,

Both eyes work independently, each one self-defined.

On Pterygium

The longer you wait to remove that growth,

The deeper the mark it leaves on your sight — that's the truth.

Cut recurrence risk with a smarter technique in play,

A fresh tissue graft keeps that growth away.

It grows slowly, so the harm's easy to miss and delay,

If redness keeps returning, it's surgery-consider day,

The longer you wait to have that growth removed,

The deeper the mark left once it's soothed.

"It's just a small growth," don't let that be your excuse,

Left too long, it reaches the pupil — no more sight to lose.

Eyes staying red again and again, with burning that won't cease,

A small surgery here brings lasting, permanent peace.

On Glaucoma

Glaucoma sneaks in with no pain, no sign,

“I see fine” is no reason to skip the line.

Like diabetes and blood pressure, it's silent and slow,

It stays for life once it starts to grow.

Never feel shame that glaucoma is yours,

Keep the drops daily — that's the one rule that endures.

Pressure builds inside the eye when glaucoma takes hold,

The optic nerve dries and thins — the story slowly told.

On Red Eyes

Red eyes come in many different kinds,

Don't self-treat — leave the diagnosis behind.

Itchy, watery eyes when seasons change,

It's just allergy — nothing too strange.

Resist the urge to rub and scratch,

Rubbing only worsens this seasonal batch.

On Spectacle Numbers

Minus for far, plus for near — the rule of thumb,

But it's not always that simple, so don't just assume.

Old glasses sitting unused in a drawer,

Could light up someone else's world once more.

Donate them at the clinic, let them serve again,

A small act of yours can ease another's strain.

Straight lines look bent, or halos form round light,

Astigmatism's the reason for that blurry sight.

Glasses fix it easily, or LASIK for good,

Don't ignore this — get checked like you should.

Clean your glasses with a soft cotton cloth,

Not your shirt hem — that coating comes off.

Rinse with water first, then wipe dust away,

Wrong cleaning habits blur your view each day.

Minus, plus, or a tilted astigmatic kind,

Whatever the number, glasses you'll need to find.

A rapidly climbing minus number in a child,

Atropine 0.01% drops can help keep it mild.

A very high number leaves the retina thin and frail,

Regular checkups matter — don't let vigilance fail.

On Digital Eye Strain

Keep two feet of distance from your screen,

Block the blue light, protect what's between.

Every hour, take a five-minute break,

Your eyes need rest — for their own sake.

Hours on mobiles and screens each day,

Raises the risk of numbers going astray.

Send them outside, into the sun's embrace,

Nature does more for eyes than any screen-based place.

On Doctor & Patient

A movie ticket often costs more than a checkup fee,

Yet people grudge doctors that easily.

One brings you joy, one takes away pain,

A doctor's fee is his bread — don't begrudge the gain.

Apply the drop without touching your eye,

Follow the doctor's schedule — don't ask why.

Red eyes with discharge — don't self-medicate,

See a doctor before it's too late.

Don't let the cost of surgery hold you back,

Insurance and installments fill that gap.

Once a bottle's opened, a month's the limit to use,

An old half-full bottle is one you should refuse.

Some drops need the fridge, don't leave them out too long,

Follow what the doctor said — that's never wrong.

If one doctor's advice doesn't sit quite right,

Ask another — it's your right, not a fight.

But don't let the search drag on for too long,

Delaying a decision can prove more wrong.

Steroid drops without a doctor's say,

Can bring glaucoma and cataract your way.

Quick relief tempts you to keep them near,

Use only as prescribed — stop when it's clear.

If glaucoma runs in your parents or kin,

Your risk is higher — the danger's within.

Tell your doctor your family's eye history plain,

Early screening catches what genes explain.

Feeling better doesn't mean the drops should stop,

Follow the full course your doctor wrote — every drop.

On Diabetic Retinopathy

If diabetes has been with you for years,

Get your retina checked — don't wait for tears.

Vision can be fine while damage creeps in slow,

Diabetic eye disease is a silent foe.

On Dry Eyes

Eyes feel gritty, burn, and water too,

Regular drops bring relief to you.

If drops alone don't bring relief in sight,

Punctal plugs can help hold moisture tight.

Using artificial tears often isn't a flaw,

Follow your doctor's guidance — no harm at all.

On Children's Eye Care

Check a child's eyes before school begins,

Delay in glasses only delays their wins.

If a child's eye turns inward or strays,

Don't laugh it off — treat it in early days.

One eye sees less, the other does all the work,

Untreated early, that gap won't budge or shirk.

Before age six is when treatment works best,

Delay makes correction a much harder test.

If red and green seem to blend as one,

Get it checked before school life has begun.

Born too early, weighing far too light,

Needs a special check to protect their sight.

The retina's growth may still be incomplete,

Early screening helps that risk retreat.

On Squint Surgery

Squint surgery's timing depends on the age,

Early in kids clears the visual stage.

Adults too can correct a lifelong squint,

There's no shame in fixing what childhood left in print.

On Sunglasses & UV

Step into the sun, wear shades that guard,

UV rays cause damage that's slow but hard.

On Regular Checkups

Past forty, make it a yearly affair,

Catch small problems before they're a bigger scare.

Once in childhood, before school starts its run,

Every two-three years in youth, checkups should be done.

Past forty, make it a yearly affair,

Past sixty, watch glaucoma and cataract with care.

On LASIK

Tired of glasses fogging up your face?

LASIK can free you — no more nose-bridge trace.

When the number's too high and LASIK's not a fit,

ICL steps in — don't give up on it.

A lens placed inside, the cornea untouched,

Even thin corneas get freedom, glasses-free and much.

The dream of a glasses-free photo, held for years,

LASIK makes it real — just one decision clears the fears.

At work, in sports, or at a wedding day,

Glasses keep getting in the way — choose freedom, come what may.

Get a proper evaluation, find out if you truly qualify,

Don't say no without knowing — decide only after asking why.

On Contact Lenses

Wear your lenses, but hygiene comes first,

Sleeping in them can make things worse.

Wearing lenses longer than the hours advised,

Starves your eyes of oxygen, infection multiplies.

Count the hours as your doctor has laid out,

Break the habit of overwear — there's no doubt.

Glasses are easier to maintain, contacts give an open view,

Each has its own upside — pick what's right for you.

For sports or special days, contacts offer real relief,

But skip the hygiene, and trouble comes to grief.

Lenses on, and itching starts with redness and a tear,

It's likely a lens-solution allergy — don't ignore it, dear.

Try switching the solution, or the lens type too,

Still no relief? See your doctor — that's the thing to do.

On Foreign Body / Chemical Injury

Something in your eye? Don't rub it in fear,

Rushing to a doctor keeps your vision clear.

Chemical splash in the eye — don't delay,

Rinse with water for fifteen minutes, then get away

To a doctor fast, don't waste more time,

Quick action here prevents a bigger crime.

An injury to the eye, redness and pain combined,

Applying home ointments yourself — leave that behind.

A corneal ulcer risks the vision you hold dear,

Get to a doctor right away — don't wait, don't fear.

On Eye Safety

Light your crackers with glasses on your face,

One careless second can leave a lifelong trace.

If a spark lands in the eye, rinse with water first,

Don't try home remedies before the worst.

Play with colors, but shield your eyes,

Chemical colors bring corneal surprise.

Weld with goggles, never skip that gear,

UV damage shows up slow, not clear.

Working fields kicks up dust and grit,

Protective glasses help you outlast it.

Monsoon season brings infections that spread,

Dirty water near the eyes is to be dreaded.

Redness or discharge that won't subside,

See a doctor — don't let it slide.

Heading for a swim? Don't forget your goggles,

Chlorinated water in the eyes causes struggles.

Stepping out where dust and smoke collect,

Wearing glasses gives your eyes some protection.

Working in a factory or workshop each day,

Don't skip safety goggles — don't put them away.

Sparks, dust, or chemicals splashing near,

One careless moment can cost you sight, my dear.

Tempted to watch the eclipse with your bare eye?

Don't — the retina burns before you know why.

Only special eclipse glasses make it safe to view,

Never through film or glass — that damage stays with you.

Cricket or badminton, a ball can come in fast,

Teach children early — safety gear should always last.

On Eye Donation & Cornea Transplant

Long after you're gone, your eyes can still see,

Through someone else's world, lit differently.

When the cornea clouds and vision grows dim,

A transplant can restore the sight within.

One donated eye, two lives made bright —

A single gift restoring double sight.

On Floaters & Retinal Detachment

Flashes of light, a curtain closing in view,

Retina detaching — act fast, it's true.

Floaters that suddenly multiply and swarm,

Don't dismiss them — get checked, avoid the harm.

Black specks drifting when you look at the sky,

Usually harmless as the years go by.

But a sudden surge, or flashes that appear,

Means it's time to get your retina checked, my dear.

Old floaters drifting are simply a normal sight,

But a sudden surge with flashes needs checking, not delight.

Learning this difference truly matters here,

When in doubt, don't wait — see your doctor, my dear.

On Presbyopia

Struggling to read past your fortieth year?

It's natural aging — nothing to fear.

On Eye Makeup & Hygiene

Wear your mascara, but remove it at night,

Leftover makeup invites infection to bite.

On Common Myths

“Wait till it ripens” — that old belief is outdated,

Small cataracts today are safely operated.

Operating one eye won't harm the other one,

Both eyes work independently, second to none.

Blue light glasses won't fix everything you fear,

They ease some strain, but aren't the cure, that's clear.

Breaks and blinking are what truly help the most,

Blue light lenses assist — but aren't the ultimate boast.

On Macular Degeneration

Straight lines look bent, or a spot blocks the view,

Age-related — early checkups can help you.

On Keratoconus

Numbers climbing fast, glasses can't quite clear,

Keratoconus could be the reason here.

Stop rubbing your eyes, especially at night,

That habit alone can worsen your sight.

For Senior Citizens

Half of home falls trace back to poor sight,

Get that cataract removed and reclaim your fight.

Age is never a reason to say no,

Surgery at eighty still helps eyes glow.

Macular degeneration or vision that's grown weak,

Don't lose hope — better living aids you can seek.

Magnifiers and special lenses help you read again,

Low vision aids support daily life, even then.

On Night Blindness

Vision fades as evening turns to dark,

Vitamin A deficiency could be the mark.

Eat your greens and carrots, keep the diet right,

Small changes now protect tomorrow's sight.

On Smoking & Eye Health

If you smoke, here's something to know,

Cataract and macular risk both grow.

Quit the habit if your eyes you'd save,

Smoke harms lungs and vision — both enslave.

On Driving License Eye Tests

Getting a driving license? Eye tests are a must,

Color vision and distance sight — both need your trust.

Hiding a weak number just to pass the test,

Risks your life and others' — don't put safety to rest.

On Hereditary Eye Disease (Retinitis Pigmentosa)

Poor night vision, and a shrinking field of view,

Could be retinitis pigmentosa — hereditary too.

If it runs in your family, get checked early on,

Genetic counseling helps guide what's to come.

On Emergency vs. Routine Checkups

If sight suddenly vanishes, or lightning flashes strike,

Rush to the doctor now — don't wait, don't delay a bit.

Chemical in the eye, or a deep injury's pain,

Every minute counts — don't hesitate again.

But if numbers shift slowly, or vision's just a bit dim,

A routine visit will do — no need to rush for him.

On Eye Nutrition

Carrots, spinach, and greens on your plate,

Build the strength your eyes appreciate.

Walnuts, almonds, flaxseed too,

Omega-3 from plants keeps tears fresh and true.

On Minor Eye Symptoms

A twitching eye isn't a sign of luck or doom,

It's tiredness or lack of sleep — rest in your room.

If it persists for weeks without a break,

Get it checked — sometimes it's more than fatigue's sake.

A sudden red patch appears out of nowhere,

No need to panic — it's usually just a small tear.

Coughing, sneezing, or blood pressure's the cause,

It fades in two weeks, following nature's laws.

Still, get it checked and your blood pressure read,

Sometimes a small sign points to something ahead.

Eyes appearing to bulge, lids that won't fully close,

Thyroid could be affecting more than one supposes.

Crust builds along the eyelid's edge each night,

Warm compresses each morning set it right.

Zigzag lines or flashes before the head starts to pound,

That's often migraine aura — no cause for alarm found.

But the first time it happens, get it checked with care,

Just to rule out other reasons that light show is there.

Eyes wanting to shut in bright light or the sun's glare,

Isn't something to brush off — many causes could be there.

On Eye Care During Pregnancy

Blood pressure through the eyes can be seen and checked too,

Diabetes or hypertension? Get your retina reviewed.

Your number may shift a little during this time,

That's normal — new glasses can wait till after, not a crime.

Once delivery is done and things settle down clear,

That's when you update your glasses, no need to fear.

On Stye (Eyelid Bump)

A swollen, painful bump appears on the lid,

It's just a stye — a common infection, nothing hid.

Don't try to pop it yourself, resist that urge,

Doctor's treatment and patience will make it submerge.

On Post-Op Care

Keep dust and dirt away from the eye,

Let no water enter till healing is nigh.

Follow the drop schedule the doctor gave,

Missing a checkup is a risk, not a save.

Years after cataract surgery, if vision turns hazy again,

It's not a new cataract — just a film, no cause for alarm.

A quick laser opens that clouded screen,

Painless, in minutes — vision bright and clean.

After cataract surgery, eye pressure can sometimes rise,

Regular checkups catch this — that's why the doctor's eyes stay wise.

On Deciding to Get Surgery

Caring for parents' eyes is a child's first duty,

A small investment protects lasting beauty — of sight, not vanity.

Ask your parents when they last had a check,

Don't let neglect put their vision at wreck.

You spend without thought on a car or a home,

Why hesitate when it's eyes of your own?

Just hearing the word "surgery" and fear takes hold?

It's a quick procedure, painless — no wound to behold.

Old stories scared you, but times have changed a lot,

Technology's come so far — there's nothing left to dread.

Your neighbor had it done and is doing fine today,

Thousands of patients have — why let yourself delay?

Don't push today's task onto tomorrow's plate,

Especially with your eyes — delay only adds weight.

On Not Falling for Free Camp Traps

Free checkups tempt with a five-minute scan,

But a rushed exam can't catch a real problem, no plan.

A proper exam checks pressure and the retina too,

No free camp has that kind of time for you.

Cheap isn't always the smarter way,

Your eyesight's worth more than what you save today.

Fearing the fee and skipping the exam is a costly mistake,

Chasing "cheap" sometimes costs the very sight at stake.

A doctor's fee is the value of their years of skill,

Don't be stingy paying it — remember this goodwill.

Free checkups sometimes come with pressure close behind,

Pushed toward quick surgery, fear-driven, unkind.

The choice and timing of surgery are yours to command,

Don't let anyone's pressure force a hurried hand.

Getting checked at just one free camp shouldn't seal the deal,

See your regular doctor too, and confirm what's real.

Village camps spring up, and rushed surgeries follow fast,

If results go wrong, who's accountable at last?

A trusted doctor nearby is worth the extra distance you'd trek,

One bad mistake costs dearly — your sight's worth every check.

Don't gamble your eyes chasing something free,

The right doctor, right exam, right fee — that's the only true guarantee.

All of these verses were written by me, purely for eye health and disease awareness. Please don't use them for self-diagnosis or self-treatment of any condition. For any eye concern, always consult a qualified eye specialist. - Dr. Dhaval Patel (MD, AIIMS Delhi)
